# Guidelines

These rules are for anyone changing this game's content, art or code, people and AI assistants alike. Several of them exist because a bug or a mistake already happened. Those are collected in [section 8](#8-rules-that-come-from-bugs-we-already-hit).

**Before you change anything,** read [HANDOFF.md](../HANDOFF.md) and [KNOWN_ISSUES.md](KNOWN_ISSUES.md).

## 1. Non-negotiables

These come from the original brief. A change that breaks one of them is not finished, however good it looks.

1. **Safety facts are real.**
   - Every action the game calls correct traces to an official source in `sources.json`: a Thai agency wherever one says it. Where only a foreign agency does (the smoke alarm, most power-cut lessons), the lesson is ⚠️ `partial`, says so, and waits for a Thai review (rule 55).
   - Never invent advice, numbers or sources. A viral post or infographic is a prompt, not a source.
   - Mark how sure you are (`verify`), and flag anything you could not confirm.
2. **Content guardrails:**
   - No drowning, deaths, injured people or photos of real damage.
   - No blaming government agencies or politicians.
   - No political, royal or religious references.
   - Humour is aimed at the situation (mud, chaos, the gecko's reactions), never at affected people.
3. **Privacy:**
   - No personal data and no cookies.
   - The only third-party script is Cloudflare Web Analytics, which is cookieless.
   - Events are anonymous counts.
   - Anything personal (scores, streaks, the home check's answers, the plan card's text, and since v1.10.0 the calculators' answers) stays in the player's own `localStorage`, and nothing a player types or reads into a tool is ever sent (rules 56 and 80).
   - The game must stay PDPA-compliant without needing a consent banner.
4. **Accessibility:**
   - Every tap target is **at least 44 px**: items, buttons, tabs, expanders and links.
   - Colour is never the only signal; use blue / orange / grey **plus** shapes, and never pair red with green.
   - Nothing flashes, and `prefers-reduced-motion` is respected.
   - Every control is reachable by keyboard and readable by a screen reader.
5. **Fast:**
   - The start screen stays **under 500 KB** compressed. It is 254 KB today (269 KB with the first game screen, v1.10.1), counting the home check's and the calculators' code, which loads only when a tool opens, and the sources' notes, which it never shows ([KNOWN_ISSUES #62](KNOWN_ISSUES.md#62-source-notes-are-downloaded-by-every-visitor)).
   - No frameworks and no runtime npm packages.
6. **Works where people share it.** It must work in the LINE, Facebook and TikTok in-app browsers on Android and iOS, in iOS Safari and in Android Chrome. Every feature that a platform may lack has a fallback (see [KNOWLEDGE §11](KNOWLEDGE.md#11-browser-and-in-app-facts)).
7. **The disclaimer and help numbers are always there.**
   - The footer reads *"เนื้อหาเพื่อการเรียนรู้ หากไม่แน่ใจให้ติดต่อช่างหรือเจ้าหน้าที่"*.
   - Every result screen lists the help numbers.
8. **Never break the live game.** `node tools/build.mjs` must pass. A content mistake fails the build, and the previous version stays live. Don't work around a validation error; fix the content.

## 2. Safety content

### 2.1 Adding or changing an item

1. **Find the source first.** Add it to `sources.json` with `agency`, `title`, `url`, `date` and `kind`:
   - **`official`:** the agency's own site. Preferred.
   - **`government`:** the agency's statement republished by กรมประชาสัมพันธ์ (prd.go.th).
   - **`secondary`:** a news article that directly quotes the agency. Use this only when the first two can't be reached, and say so in `note`.
   - **`academic`:** a peer-reviewed study or an academic statement. Flag the item ⚠️ `partial`.
   - **`international`:** a UN, WHO or foreign agency page (WHO Europe, the US Fire Administration, the US CDC, Ready.gov, the Tokyo Metropolitan Government). Use it to cross-check Thai advice. When it is the *only* source, the item is ⚠️ `partial` with a `verifyNote` that says so (today: `f-alarm` and seven power-cut lessons; the list is under *Flags* in [01-safety-content.md](01-safety-content.md#flags-please-review-these)). Quote what you read in the source's `note`, with the date you read it.
2. **Write the item in `items.json`** (field reference: [KNOWLEDGE §5](KNOWLEDGE.md#5-content-model)):
   - **One correct choice**, using the agency's action in plain words.
   - **One or two wrong choices** that are *common real mistakes*. Each has gentle `feedback` that says why it's risky, and gives the reason a real person would give for doing it (v1.9.2, rule 75), in at most 60 Thai letters or 80 English characters.
   - A **tip** of **80 characters or fewer** in Thai *and* English. It is one action, starting with the verb.
   - A **"ทำไม?"** (`why`) text that says which agency recommends this and why, in one or two sentences.
3. **Set `verify` honestly:**
   - `verified`: the agency's wording supports the action as written.
   - `partial`: the core is official but one detail is inferred, older, or from another body. Explain which in `verifyNote`.
   - `unverified`: no official source. The build warns. Do not ship these.
4. Run `npm run docs` to regenerate the table in [01-safety-content.md](01-safety-content.md). Then run `node tools/build.mjs` and `npm test`.
5. If the item is in a playable room, check that it fits a slot and doesn't crowd a neighbour (see [KNOWN_ISSUES #1](KNOWN_ISSUES.md#fixed)).

### 2.2 Numbers

- **Verify every number** against its source: phone numbers, distances, days, ratios and temperatures.
- **A number a tool computes with** (the home check's amounts, the fridge's hours, the dust bands) is in `kit.json` next to its sources, and the build stops unless it is in one of their titles or notes. Judge the status on the exact figure and round only what the screen shows (no rounding may make it look safer), add no margin of your own, and flag a foreign figure ⚠️ on screen (rule 78).
- **When sources disagree, leave the number out of the game** and record the conflict under *Flags* in 01-safety-content.md. Current exclusions: sandbag fill, bleach dilution, boiling time, mattress disposal, and from the stockpiling infographic (v1.7.0) the 7-day target, a 3–6-month check and an eating order for the fridge, freezer and cans ([KNOWLEDGE §27.3](KNOWLEDGE.md#273-what-was-left-out-and-why)).
- Re-check every **hotline** before each season. A wrong number that was deliberately left out: 192 (use 1784). 199 (fire) was left out of the flood version because only news reports cited it; the fire mode (v1.5.0) added it, quoting DDPM through a news report.

### 2.3 Wrong answers and feedback

- Wrong options must be **plausible**: what people actually do. They must never be stunts that would be dangerous to copy because the game made them look normal.
- **A wrong option says why someone would do it** ("ปล่อยไว้ในลิ้นชักเหมือนเดิม เสียก็ไปขอทำใหม่ได้"), and keeps the exact mistake its `feedback` answers. Don't make it half right ("leave them plugged in but switched off", when the source says "turn off or disconnect"), and add no fact, advice or number.
- **Nothing on the button may give the answer away:** not its length, either way (the right answer can't always be the longest, nor never), and not an icon (the buttons are lettered ก ข ค since v1.9.2, and `kind` isn't drawn). `npm test` checks the lengths ([rule 75](#8-rules-that-come-from-bugs-we-already-hit), [KNOWLEDGE §31.1](KNOWLEDGE.md#311-wrong-answers-that-dont-give-the-right-one-away)).
- Feedback explains the risk **without blame or fear**: *"ไม่เป็นไร รู้แล้วรอดของจริง"*. The status is "learned" (orange !), never "failed".
- No rescue procedures that show an injured person, and no depth thresholds for walking in water.

### 2.4 Changing advice that is already public

- **Never edit safety advice quietly.**
  - After the deploy, post a correction that starts with **"UPDATE:"** on every channel where the game was shared.
  - Say what changed and why.
- **Update the source dates.** If the guidance changed, also update the "ตรวจสอบเมื่อ" date in the About sheet. It is currently in `main.js`; see [KNOWN_ISSUES #5](KNOWN_ISSUES.md#fixed).
- **Ship structural edits at night.** Adding, removing or disabling items, or changing slots, reshuffles today's house. Ship such edits around midnight Bangkok time ([KNOWN_ISSUES #24](KNOWN_ISSUES.md#24-some-content-edits-reshuffle-todays-house-and-old-challenge-links)).

### 2.5 Seasonal review (April–May, before the rains)

Follow the checklist in [04-launch-kit.md §5](04-launch-kit.md#5-relaunch-every-rainy-season):

1. Re-check every source and hotline.
2. Update the dates.
3. Bump `config.houseVersion`.
4. Re-render the images.
5. Re-run the full test matrix.

## 3. Thai and English copy and tone

- **Voice:** a helpful neighbour. Warm, casual and native. Short sentences, with particles used naturally (นะ, จ้า, เลย). Explain jargon; use everyday words (ภาษาชาวบ้าน).
- **Tips:**
  - one action per tip, verb first, 80 characters or fewer
  - no exclamation-mark shouting
  - no "you must or you will die" framing
- **The gecko's lines:** about 30 Thai characters or fewer so they fit the speech bubble in one or two lines. Encouraging, never sarcastic about the player.
- **Rank titles** flatter at every level. Even the lowest rank (*มือใหม่หัดเตรียมตัว*) is something people are happy to share.
- **Humour:** at the situation only. Mud, the rope that isn't a snake, a gecko in boots. Never at victims, rescuers, agencies or any area of the country.
- **Avoid:**
  - names of politicians, parties or party colours
  - royal or religious references
  - catchphrases that mock officials, even popular ones
  - fear-mongering
  - AI-generated or realistic disaster imagery
- **English** mirrors the tone. It is written for visitors from outside Thailand and for helpers, not as a separate voice.
  - **One variety: British English** (rule 58). "torch", not "flashlight"; "power cut", not "power outage" or "blackout"; "litres", "nappies", "colour". When a source is American (Ready.gov, the US CDC, the US Fire Administration), quote it in `sources.json → note` as written, but write the lesson in the game's words; agency names keep their own spelling. Before a release, search the `en` text of every content file for the American words.
  - One name for one thing: the home check is about "our home" in its title, buttons, card and share text, not "house" in some and "home" in others. Since v1.9.1 each tool has one name everywhere: เช็กของจำเป็น / "Check your supplies" and แผนครอบครัว / "Our family plan" ([KNOWN_ISSUES F68](KNOWN_ISSUES.md#in-v191-19-oct-2026)); since v1.10.0 also ไฟดับ: ตู้เย็น / "Power cut: fridge" (its sheet "ไฟดับ: ของในตู้เย็นยังกินได้ไหม?") and ฝุ่นวันนี้ / "Dust today" ("ฝุ่นวันนี้ บ้านเราต้องทำอะไร?"). The hub, the guides' next steps and the result buttons all take the name from `hub<Id>`.
  - Every player-facing string has a `th` and an `en` value with the same keys (281 each in `strings.json` since v1.10.0, and 97 in `kit.json → ui` since v1.10.1), and every `og` key has an `ogEn` twin. The build fails otherwise.
  - Say plainly that the hotlines are Thai and that most advice comes from Thai agencies; never say *all* of it does (rule 55). Don't add foreign numbers or laws that no source backs.
  - Agency names appear in English in `seo.en.agencies`.
  - The wordmark is `logoA` + `logoB` in each language ("Baan " + "Rod Mai?" in English; a space at the end of `logoA` is intentional). Never hard-code the name in HTML or canvas code.
- **Thai line breaks:** after adding or changing Thai text, look at it at 320 and 360 px. If a word breaks across two lines ("กระ|จก"), add the whole word to `config.json → keepTogether` (rule 68); never put invisible characters into the text.
- **Formatting:**
  - Keep `{placeholders}` exactly as they are.
  - Write phone numbers as plain digits (`1784`).
  - Write Thai dates as `6 ต.ค. 69` in the UI (Buddhist era, short), produced by `formatHouseDate()`.
- **Review:** have a second native speaker read new copy before release, ideally an older family member from the forwarding audience, and a native English reader for English copy (the owner has promised a language check, [KNOWN_ISSUES #11](KNOWN_ISSUES.md#11-thai-and-english-copy-need-a-second-native-reader)).

## 4. Art

- **Hand-made SVG only:** flat and warm, with chunky outlines (`#4A3426`, stroke about 2–2.2). Use the palette in `css/app.css` `:root`: mud browns plus sun yellow, sky blue, coral and teal. No photos, no AI images, and no people in danger.
- **Room files** (`public/art/rooms/<room>.svg`):
  - `viewBox="0 0 360 600"`.
  - Top-level layers in this order:
    1. `<g class="bg">`, with `sky-rain` and `sky-sun` inside
    2. `<g class="mud">`, the Return look
    3. `<g class="items">`, which the game fills
    4. `<g class="water">`, Prepare only
  - **Paint order is document order.** Later elements cover earlier ones.
  - Prefix every `id` with the room (`fr-`, `lv-`, `kt-`, and so on). All rooms are inlined into one page, so ids would otherwise clash.
- **Sprites** (`public/art/sprites.svg`): one `<symbol id="s-<name>">` each. `size` in `items.json` sets the drawn size.
  - Draw a "done" state (for example `breaker-off`) when the right action visibly changes the object.
  - Test icons at 24 px; an hourglass once read as "Σ".
- **Slots** (`rooms.json`):
  - `(x, y)` is the **bottom centre** of whatever sits there, and tags say what fits.
  - Items on the same baseline need **at least about 70 scene units** between slot centres, so their 64-unit tap areas don't overlap.
  - Run `npm run probe` after moving slots. It reports overlapping tap areas and flood timings.
- **Status colours:** blue `--ok` ✓, orange `--warn` !, grey `--miss` ? or ~. Red and green are never paired.

## 5. Code

- **Plain ES modules in `public/js/`, served as they are.**
  - No bundler, no transpiler, no framework.
  - The only npm package is the dev-only `playwright-core`, for images and smoke tests.
  - The build uses only Node built-ins.
- **Logic stays pure.** `house.js`, `round.js`, `rng.js`, `run.js`, `modes.js` and `kitplan.js` (the home check's amounts and score, v1.8.0) don't touch the DOM. Tests and the build import them in Node. Keep it that way, and put DOM work in `scene.js`, `ui.js` and `main.js`.
- **Data, not code.**
  - Tunable numbers go in `config.json`.
  - Text players see goes in `strings.json`, `items.json` and `checklists.json`, and the home check's and plan card's in `kit.json`. A few exceptions remain; see [KNOWN_ISSUES #5](KNOWN_ISSUES.md#fixed), and don't add more.
  - New features go behind `config.features.*`.
- **Tap targets:**
  - Items get `MIN_HIT` (64 scene units).
  - Controls get CSS `min-height` / `min-width` of 44 px. That includes the short-screen media query.
  - Inline text links get padding with negative margins: a 44 px tall tap area without moving the text.
  - The smoke test measures all of these.
- **In-app browsers:** check that a feature exists before using it, and always have a fallback. Never rely on any of these:
  - `window.open`
  - downloads
  - `navigator.share`
  - service workers
  - `navigator.vibrate`
  - clipboard access outside a tap
- **Network:**
  - Gameplay never waits on the network.
  - Analytics are fire-and-forget (`sendBeacon`).
  - Optional calls such as `forecastStatusUrl` have short timeouts (1.5 s).
- **Storage:** only through `store` in `util.js` (prefix `brm:`, wrapped in try/catch). Private mode and blocked storage must not break the game.
- **Accessibility:**
  - SVG items use `role="button"`, `tabindex="0"` and an `aria-label`, and Enter and Space work on them.
  - Sheets trap focus and close with Esc, their ✕ and, since v1.9.1, the phone's back button (rule 67). Since v1.9.2 Back in a round pauses it and on the result goes to the start screen (rule 77).
  - Motion goes through CSS animations that the reduced-motion query turns off.
- **Generated files** are never edited by hand: `public/sw.js` (edit `tools/sw.template.js`), `public/js/version.js`, `public/c/*.html`, `public/checklist/*.html`, the English pages under `public/en/`, the SEO files `public/learn.html`, `robots.txt`, `sitemap.xml` and `llms.txt`, the hazard pages `public/<slug>.html` and `public/en/<slug>.html` (`/flood`, `/pm25`, `/fire`, `/ready`, `/landslide`, `/storm`, `/quake`, `/heat`; edit `tools/build.mjs`, `strings.json → seo` or the content JSON), `public/kit.html`, `public/plan.html`, `public/posts.html`, `public/404.html` (v1.9.1), and in `index.html` the `<!--OG:START-->` block (with the canonical link and JSON-LD), the `<title>`, the meta description, the static Thai text and, since v1.9.1, the alert card (`#alert-card`).
- **Search and AI search.**
  - The lessons on `/learn`, the hazard pages and `/llms.txt` come from the content JSON. Never hand-write lesson text elsewhere.
  - A hazard page's address is its slug in `config.json → hazards`. **Never change a slug once published:** shared links and search results point at it. Hazard pages have their own canonical link and are in the sitemap, like `/learn`.
  - Bump `strings.json → seo.updated` (YYYY-MM-DD) whenever lessons or sources change.
  - Keep every crawl page script-free, and every link or button on it at least 44 px tall.
  - Add structured data only for things that exist on the page. No `FAQPage`, `HowTo`, ratings or reviews unless the page really has them.
  - Share-preview pages stay out of the sitemap. The challenge pages (`/c/*`) keep a canonical link to `/` (`/en/` for the English ones); since v1.9.1 a checklist page (`/checklist/<id>`) points its canonical at its hazard's page, which has the same list as text, and has no `hreflang`.
  - Keep `/api/` the only path `robots.txt` closes, and leave Cloudflare's AI-bot blocking off for this host.
- **Budget:** read the build's `budget` line after any change that adds files, fonts or JS. The start screen limit is 500 KB, and the aim is to stay near today's 243 KB (each mode's content adds to it, about 12 KB for the power cut; the four guides of v1.9.0 and their 36 sources added 23 KB, mostly notes the game never shows; v1.9.1's hub text, guide sheet and `keepTogether` list 8 KB; v1.9.2's longer wrong answers, history manager and card 3 KB). The line counts every file in `js/`, even code loaded on demand such as `kit.js` ([KNOWN_ISSUES #59](KNOWN_ISSUES.md#59-the-home-checks-code-is-counted-in-the-start-screen-budget-and-precached)). A tool the start screen doesn't need is loaded with `import()` when it opens, and its text lives in its own file.
- **Style:** follow the surrounding code. Two-space indentation, single quotes, semicolons, small functions, and comments that explain *why*.

Rules:

- **Rule changes get a unit test,** in `tests/run.mjs`.

## 7. Git and releases

- **Branches.**
  - `main` is production: Cloudflare deploys every push.
  - Every other branch gets its own preview URL.
  - Content typo fixes may go straight to `main` through the GitHub editor, because the build protects the site. Code changes go through a branch.
- **Commit messages:** `feat:`, `fix:`, `content:`, `docs:`, `test:`, `chore:`. Say *what* and *why*. Content commits name the item ids.
- **Generated files.** Commit them as `node tools/build.mjs` produced them. Cloudflare rebuilds them on every deploy, so they only need to be valid ([KNOWN_ISSUES #27](KNOWN_ISSUES.md#27-the-build-rewrites-tracked-files)).
- **Images are the exception.** Cloudflare can't render them, so commit `public/og/`, `public/share/` and `public/icons/` re-rendered with the real `SITE_URL`.
- **Secrets:** never commit any. The Cloudflare API token lives in `.env` (git-ignored); `.env.example` shows the format and the permissions it needs. Keep `.dev.vars`, API tokens and account ids out of git; `.gitignore` covers the usual files. The game itself needs no secrets.
- **Versions.** Use semantic versions in `package.json`, with a matching `CHANGELOG.md` entry and a `vX.Y.Z` tag on `main` after the deploy is checked.
- **Release checklist:**
  1. `node tools/build.mjs` passes with no unexpected warnings.
  2. `npm test` and `npm run smoke` pass.
  3. Images are re-rendered if the copy, ranks, checklists or domain changed (Thai `og/` and English `og/en/`). Restore any PNG you did not mean to change with `git checkout -- <path>`; a different Chromium alters every PNG slightly. Compare pixels, not bytes, to tell a real change from noise ([LESSONS E10](LESSONS_LEARNED.md#e-process-documentation-and-git)).
  4. `CHANGELOG.md` and the version are updated. The version lives in `package.json` (and `package-lock.json`); `config.json → version` must match it, and the build fails if it doesn't.
  5. Merge to `main`, and wait for the Cloudflare deploy.
  6. Open the live site once and reload, so the new service worker takes over. Check that a changed text appears, in Thai (`/`) **and** English (`/en/`), and that the version chip matches. A green push is not a live site.
  7. If the previews changed, re-scrape them in Facebook's Sharing Debugger and check LINE with `?v=<n>`.
  8. If safety content changed, publish an "UPDATE:" post. Bump `strings.json → seo.updated`.
  9. If a version tag is wanted, the owner creates it as a GitHub Release, because the session's git proxy refuses tag pushes.
- **Rollback:** Cloudflare → the Pages project → Deployments → pick an earlier one → *Rollback*.
- **Prefer additive history.**
  - Fix mistakes with new commits (reverts, removal commits), not resets.
  - Force-push only when the owner explicitly asks, with `--force-with-lease=<branch>:<expected sha>`, and check the result with `git ls-remote`.
  - Branch deletion is done by the owner in the GitHub UI.
- **Before committing,** read `git status --short` and stage files by name, not with `git add -A`. CI fails only on generated files under `public/` that were left uncommitted; a stray file that does get committed (a probe's screenshot, as in v1.7.0) passes it ([LESSONS E8](LESSONS_LEARNED.md#e-process-documentation-and-git)). Run one-off probes from the session scratchpad.
- **Before pushing,** check that a fresh clone builds with a clean `git status` and passes `npm test`.

## 8. Rules that come from bugs we already hit

Each rule encodes a mistake that was made once. Details are under *Fixed* in [KNOWN_ISSUES.md](KNOWN_ISSUES.md#fixed).

1. **No control under 44 px, including in media queries.** Compact layouts for short screens once shrank the HUD and tabs to 40 px.
2. **Expanders and inline links are tap targets too.** "ทำไม?" and the source links were 21–40 px until they were measured.
3. **Check `document.readyState` before waiting for `load`.** Boot ran after `load` had fired, so the service worker never registered.
4. **One class name, one meaning.** `.mode-prepare` on both the mode buttons and the app root turned the result screen blue. Root state now uses `is-prepare` / `is-return`.
5. **"Under water" means the sprite's centre**, which matches what players see in the perspective art. Whether the floor is wet is a separate threshold (`floorWetY`).
6. **In-game messages go in the gecko's bubble** (`say()`, top-right). Toasts are for app-level messages and must never cover the floor where items sit.
7. **SVG paint order is document order.** Draw the wall before the lamp and the ground before the steps, and look at a screenshot after any art change.
8. **Use `data:` image URLs in in-app browsers.** `blob:` URLs failed to display or save there.

Write a smoke check that opens a page before it, as a chat would, presses Back (`page.goBack()`) and checks that the visitor is still on the site and sees what the rule says ([KNOWLEDGE §31.2](KNOWLEDGE.md#312-the-back-button-one-history-manager)).
78. **A calculator computes only from sourced numbers, and no rounding makes an answer look safer.** The home check (v1.8.0) and the calculators of v1.10.0 (the fridge in a power cut, dust today, the pills) turn a family's answers into amounts, hours and colours, so a made-up or mistyped figure would reach every family that uses them. Every figure a calculator computes with lives in `kit.json` beside its sources, and must appear in a cited source's title or `note`: the build checks the home check's amounts (rule 57), the fridge's hours and every dust band edge, and was shown to fail on a wrong one (the fridge at 5 h, orange at 70 µg/m³, green at AQI 10). A figure only a foreign agency gives is ⚠️ `partial`, and the tool says so on screen, in the text it shares and on its page ("ตัวเลขชั่วโมงมาจาก CDC และ Ready.gov ของสหรัฐฯ ยังไม่พบคำแนะนำเรื่องนี้จากหน่วยงานไทย", rule 55). Add no margin, factor or threshold of your own. Never let rounding make the answer look safer than its source: judge the status (✅ ⏳ ❌, a dust colour) on the exact figure, or on the figure rounded the way the source itself rounds it (the notice's one decimal, half up), never on what the screen shows; round what the screen shows only for reading ("ราว", to the nearest minute, and in whole hours from 3 hours, since v1.10.1); and count a part pill as a whole one. v1.10.0 rounded the time off up and the time left down instead, so a count just started read "1 นาที" and a time just tapped "3 ชม. 59 นาที": safe, but numbers a visitor reads as wrong (F94). A button that changes a value changes it by exactly its step from what is shown (F92: `Math.round` turned 3 h 30 min + 1 into 5 h). Put the sums in a pure function (`kitplan.js`) with a unit test, and cross-check them with a script written apart from it ([KNOWLEDGE §32](KNOWLEDGE.md#32-calculators-the-fridge-dust-today-the-pills-v1100), [LESSONS B13, C23 and D21](LESSONS_LEARNED.md#d-testing-and-verification)).
79. **Ask the unit; don't guess it.** The same number means different things in different units: on Thailand's scale AQI 152 is orange and 152 µg/m³ is red, and the US AQI in many weather apps is another scale again. Dust today asks which kind of number it is (µg/m³ or Thailand's AQI), remembers the answer, names the scale on screen ("ตัวเลข AQI (แอปของไทย)") and tells people with a foreign app to put in the PM2.5 number instead. Say what the bands are defined on (the 24-hour average, [KNOWN_ISSUES #75](KNOWN_ISSUES.md#75-the-dust-colours-are-for-the-24-hour-average-apps-often-show-hourly-readings)), and offer a way in that needs no number: since v1.10.1 the colour the app shows is the first way in, and the number waits behind "🔢 รู้ตัวเลข? ใส่ตัวเลขแทน" (rule 82). A later input with a unit (°C or °F, litres or bottles, hours or a clock time) asks the same way ([LESSONS A7](LESSONS_LEARNED.md#a-product-and-audience)).
80. **Answers are given once and reused; nothing typed or read is sent.** A family shouldn't answer "who is at home" in every tool, and nothing it answers should leave the phone. A new tool starts from the answers it can reuse (dust today ticks young children, older people and a long-term illness from the home check's `baby`, `elder` and `meds`, through `groups[].from`, which the build checks), lets the person change them, and keeps its own in `localStorage` through `store` (`brm:fridge`, `brm:air`). Keep only what the next visit needs: the fridge keeps when the power went off, so the count goes on after the page is closed; dust today keeps the unit and the ticks, never the reading. Events carry the tool and a label from a fixed list (where it was opened from, the share channel), never a value: no reading, time, count of people or answer (`tool_open`, `tool_share`), and a smoke check reads every `/api/e` body for the value typed. This takes rule 56 from what is typed to what is read and worked out.
81. **When two agencies differ, one is the main text and the other is named on its line.** The PCD's notice and the DOH give the same PM2.5 bands but different advice at green, yellow and red ([KNOWN_ISSUES #76](KNOWN_ISSUES.md#76-the-pcd-and-the-doh-advise-differently-at-green-yellow-and-red)). Choose the main text for a reason you can write down (here: the legal notice, the one Air4Thai follows, which separates the general public from people at risk), add the other agency's lines only where they add something, and name that agency on the line, on screen and on the page ("(กรมอนามัย)", "— Department of Health (DOH)"). Never blend two sets' lines for the same people at the same level into one unnamed list, and never choose a line because it is milder or stricter. Record the difference and the choice in the sources' `note`s and in KNOWLEDGE, so a reviewer can reverse it ([KNOWLEDGE §32.3](KNOWLEDGE.md#323-dust-today), [LESSONS B14](LESSONS_LEARNED.md#b-safety-content-and-research)). Where a number differs, not only the wording, §2.2 still applies: leave the number out.
82. **Tools in plain words: one question at a time, the answer as a word first, the source once.** The owner, shown the v1.10.0 fridge tool, wrote: "I cannot understand, please review and validate the UI and UX, try to improve and use general simple language" (F93). The tool had passed 157 smoke checks and an independent cross-check, so it was right, and still unreadable: it counted "1 นาที" before anyone had said the power was off, offered two ways to set the time (− / + and "ไฟเพิ่งดับ") with no hint which to use, packed the time left, a condition and a clock time with a date into each line, repeated a long agency name under every row, and put words such as "ช่องธรรมดา" and "ระบบนับต่อให้เองแม้ปิดหน้านี้" first, with the freezer question below the answers it changed. So a tool asks one question at a time, and puts a follow-up next to the answer it changes (the freezer's fill inside the freezer's card); offers taps rather than typing where it can (9 time buttons, 5 colours), with one way in first and the other behind a "know the number?" section; starts nothing (no count, no stored answer) before the person asks, and says where the answer will appear ("👆 แตะเวลาด้านบน แล้วคำตอบจะขึ้นตรงนี้"); answers with a status word first, large ("ยังกินได้", "ใกล้ครบ 4 ชม.", "เกิน 4 ชม.แล้ว"), then one line, with no clock time, date or second condition stacked on it; uses no jargon up front ("ระบบ", "ช่องธรรมดา", a scale's name before the person knows what it is); and says its sources once, at the bottom, with the ⚠️ note, while its page (`/fridge`, `/air`) keeps every line's sources. Sourced advice may be put in everyday words only when the meaning, the strength and the source stay the same: the notice's reduce, limit, avoid and stop stay four different words (ให้น้อยลง, ให้สั้นลง, เลี่ยง, งด), a DOH line stays named (rule 81), and a rewording a native reader might doubt is listed for them ([KNOWLEDGE §32.10](KNOWLEDGE.md#3210-the-plain-words-pass-v1101)). Before calling a tool done, read it as a first-time older visitor on a 360 × 640 screen, in Thai, with nothing answered yet, and write the smoke checks for the taps a person really makes ([LESSONS A8](LESSONS_LEARNED.md#a-product-and-audience)).
83. **A calculator never tells anyone to throw away medicine.** v1.10.0–v1.10.1 said "❌ เกิน 1 วันแล้ว · Ready.gov แนะให้ทิ้ง" for any medicine that must be kept cold, after a day without power. For insulin, the commonest such medicine at home, the US FDA says up to 28 days out of the fridge at 15–30 °C, so the tool could have made someone throw away medicine they could not replace that night; both visitor audits of v1.11.0 caught it, and it was their only blocker (F96). A tool that turns time into a verdict on medicine gives the condition first and the action a person can take: insulin keeps up to 28 days at 15–30 °C, out of the sun and never frozen (`fda-insulin`, with `siriraj-insulin`); for other medicine, check the label, and if it doesn't say it can be kept out of the fridge, ask a pharmacist before using it; a medicine someone can't do without stays in use until a new supply comes (`fda-drugs-disaster`). Its status word is never "ทิ้ง": past its time it is "check" (🔎, `overAs: 'check'`, the only value the build allows), and the unit test fails on a medicine status that says ทิ้ง. Where no source gives a number (insulin above 30 °C), say what happens without one ("ยาจะค่อยๆ เสื่อม") and add none of your own ([KNOWN_ISSUES #80](KNOWN_ISSUES.md#80-insulin-above-30-c-has-no-number)). The same goes for any lesson or guide that quotes a "discard" line about medicine: the game's `s-medcold` did, in its `why` and a wrong answer's feedback, since v1.7.0, and was reworded in v1.11.0 ([F128](KNOWN_ISSUES.md#in-v1110-20-oct-2026)); search the content for ทิ้งยา and "discard" after any change to medicine advice; a Thai pharmacist reads the wording before it is promoted ([LESSONS B16](LESSONS_LEARNED.md#b-safety-content-and-research)).
84. **A verdict per item comes from a named row of a chart; a row that doesn't fit Thailand is left out, not stretched.** The fridge's food lists (v1.11.0) give each kind of food the verdict of one row of FoodSafety.gov's or USDA FSIS's charts (`after: discard | keep`; in the freezer `crystals` and `thawed`: `refreeze | discard`), with that row's sources; the build stops on any other value, on a food with no source, and unless there are foods both to throw out and to keep. The Thai name is ours, so the reviewer gets the mapping (ลูกชิ้น and หมูยอ under "lunchmeats, hot dogs, sausage") and the doubtful ones flagged (eggs). A row that would mislead in a Thai kitchen stays out: the chart's "Fish sauces, oyster sauce: Discard" would have told families to throw out their น้ำปลา, a cupboard condiment, and the opposite has no source; so would rows the sources disagree on (opened fruit juice, coconut) or that need a thermometer (mayonnaise). What is left out is listed with the reason ([KNOWLEDGE §33.4](KNOWLEDGE.md#334-what-was-left-out-and-the-flags-for-the-reviewer)) and kept out by a unit test, and the tool says, under every list, "ของที่ไม่อยู่ในรายการ ไม่แน่ใจให้ทิ้ง" (anything not on the list: if in doubt, throw it out), the sources' own rule. Keep the time and the verdict apart: the fridge's 4 hours decide when, the row decides what; never add a chart's thermometer time ("above 4 °C for more than 2 hours") to the fridge's hours ([LESSONS B15](LESSONS_LEARNED.md#b-safety-content-and-research)).
85. **Every phone number is shown with whom it reaches, and a guide shows the numbers that fit it.** Until v1.11.0 every guide showed 1784, 199 and 1669 as bare numbers, so the power-cut guide had no electricity number and nothing said what each number was for (F106). Every number in `helplines.json` has a `short` name in Thai and English (the build checks it), shown under the number wherever a number is drawn: the guide's chips, its print footer, its picture (`drawCalls()`), the hazard pages' call row and the plan card. Each guide lists its own numbers in `config.json → guides[].calls`, 1 to 4, each one in `helplines.json` (the build checks both): the power cut 1130 and 1129 first, a fire 199 first. Keep the short names short enough for a 320 px screen, and when a short name has to stand for more than it says ("power: Bangkok area" for MEA's three provinces), record it for the reader ([KNOWN_ISSUES #82](KNOWN_ISSUES.md#82-power-bangkok-area-stands-for-meas-three-provinces)). Re-render the guide pictures after a change. Since v1.13.1 a number is always a button to call, never plain text: heat today's "โทร 1669 ทันที" was text until visitor audit 4 (F132); the tools draw numbers with `telChips()` in `kit.js`.
86. **Advice for the reader's own group is never hidden behind a tick.** Dust today showed its lines for young children, older people, pregnant women and people with a long-term illness only after someone ticked who was at home; with nobody ticked, a hint said more advice existed. But the person reading is often the one at risk, an older relative or someone with asthma, and a hint is one more thing to understand (F101). Show the lines for the groups at risk always, after the lines for everyone, under a heading that names the groups; give only what is new for them ("ทำตามข้อด้านบน และ" and then the extra lines), never a line twice, and "ทำแบบเดียวกับทุกคน" when there is nothing more. A tick may change what is first or what is sent, never whether safety advice is shown. The unit test checks that no level repeats a line and that the at-risk lines come with no tick.
87. **A share button says what it really sends; when the picture can't go, the text doesn't pretend it did.** The plan card's share text said "แผนครอบครัวของบ้านเรา 📝 เซฟไว้ในมือถือทุกคนนะ", but sent through LINE's or Facebook's share link it arrived as text alone, with no plan, and "คัดลอกลิงก์" copied a whole message (F112, F115). Name a button after what it does ("คัดลอกข้อความ", "Copy message"); write a share text that is true with or without the picture ("ทำการ์ดแผนครอบครัวของบ้านเราแล้ว 📝 ดูรูปการ์ดที่ส่งในกลุ่ม…"); put how to send the picture at the top of an image's share sheet ("👆 วิธีส่งทั้งการ์ด: กดค้างที่รูป…"); and where people need the content itself in a chat, offer it as text on the clipboard: "📋 คัดลอกแผนเป็นข้อความ", "🛒 คัดลอกรายการที่ยังขาด". What people typed or answered goes only to the clipboard, never into a URL, an event or a request (rule 56). A shared text that reports a time says the clock time, because it is read later: "ไฟดับตั้งแต่ราว 21:10 น. (3 ชม.แล้ว)" (F100).
88. **Kept Thai words never sit directly in a flex or grid box; use `inline-block`.** The static pages' links (`.top a`, `.check a`, `.tix a`, `.jump a`) were `inline-flex` so that they could be 44 px tall. Each kept Thai word is a `.nw` span (rule 68), and in a flex box every span and every run of text between spans became a flex item of its own: the words stood in columns and the spaces between them collapsed ("🎒 เช็ก | ของจำเป็น |", "รับมือพายุลมแรงฟ้าผ่า10 ข้อ"), in 45 links (F122). Make a text link 44 px tall with `display: inline-block`, padding and a line height, not with flex; if a box must be flex or grid, wrap its whole text in one element so the box has one child for it. This is rule 72's other half: rule 72 stops CSS rules from catching the spans, this one stops the box around them from splitting the line. After a change to a link or a button that holds Thai, read it at 320 px in both languages, or dump each line's text.
89. **Check a class name for collisions before using it.** The power-cut guide's new button (v1.11.0) was first given the class `guide-now`, which the guide picker already uses for its "ช่วงนี้" badge (`position: absolute`, 12 px), so the button took the badge's style (F127). Before adding a class, search the CSS and the scripts for it (`grep -n 'guide-now' public/css public/js`), name it for what it is (`guide-now-btn`), and give each class one meaning only (LESSONS C6, [C24](LESSONS_LEARNED.md#c-engineering-and-platform)). A screenshot of the new element, not only the text check, shows the collision.
90. **A scripted edit checks that its anchor occurs exactly once.** v1.13.0's build checks were added by a script that spliced text in after `const T = kitFile.tank;`; the same line already stood earlier in the file, so about 690 lines of `build.mjs` were copied twice, and the copy had to be cut out before the commit. Before any scripted replace or insert, assert the anchor's count is 1 (`assert s.count(anchor) == 1`), and after it compare `git diff --stat` with the size of the change you meant; an Edit with a unique `old_string` does this by itself ([LESSONS E12](LESSONS_LEARNED.md#e-process-documentation-and-git)).
91. **An agency link is a deep https page or an exact LINE id, with the date it was checked.** Government sites move pages and some open on a splash page first, so the all-tools page (v1.12.0) links the page that does the job (the TMD's warnings, not its home page), and a LINE account by its id (`@linealert`). Every link in `kit.json → links` has `checked`; the build refuses http, a LINE id that isn't `@` and letters or digits, and the home pages it knows, and warns after 120 days. Run `npm run links` monthly and open what it reports as ERR or BLK on a phone before deciding a page has gone ([KNOWN_ISSUES #88](KNOWN_ISSUES.md#88-agency-links-can-move-they-are-checked-by-hand-monthly)). Never link an app-store page: two stores need two links, and LINE's browser may not leave for one.
92. **A fire at home is never a mask question.** In the mask tool (v1.12.0) "a fire at home" answers "หนีออกจากบ้าน" with DDPM's escape lines, 199 and the fire guide, and nothing else: no mask, no card, nothing to share, because any step between reading and leaving costs time. A nearby factory fire answers "move away: a mask can't filter it". When a tool covers a situation where the right action is to leave, that is its whole answer.
93. **Tools are listed once, in `config.json → tools`.** The app's `TOOLS`, the build's `TOOL_IDS`, `/tools`, the hub's tiles (`hub.season`) and each hazard's `tools` read that list, and the build stops on a tile or a hazard naming a tool that isn't in it. Tests read the count from the list too: two smoke checks had the count of tools and one hazard's tools written in (4, and the quake's three), and failed the day a tool was added. Adding a tool means one entry there, its sheet in `kit.js` (`open<Id>`), its page and preview, and its content in `kit.json` with its sources.
94. **Read every tool in English with one person.** For a household of one the English tools said "1 people at home" and "(3 L × 1 people × 3 days)" from v1.8.0 to v1.13.0, and nobody saw it, because every smoke check ran in Thai, which has no plural, with three or four people (F130). `u()` in `kit.js` now makes "1 person", "1 day", "1 litre", "1 pill" in English; write a new counted text with the plural noun after the number ("{n} days") so the rule catches it, and a name that starts a button goes in lower case inside a sentence (`midSentence()`, "About 100 ml of liquid chlorine"). The visitor walk of a new tool includes English, and its smoke checks include one person ([LESSONS D26](LESSONS_LEARNED.md#d-testing-and-verification)).
95. **A number typed by hand is read the way people type it, and an implausible one is asked about, not answered.** The tank tool (v1.13.0) dropped everything but digits, so "1.5" became 15 litres and 2 ml of chlorine, while someone who meant 1.5 คิว (cubic metres, as the MWA's leaflet counts) needed 150 ml (F151). Read decimals with a point or a comma ("1.5", "1,5"), keep "1,500" as fifteen hundred (`typedNumber()` in `kit.js`), use `inputmode="decimal"`, and when the number can't be what the tool is for (a tank under 100 litres), ask ("หมายถึง 1.5 คิว (1,500 ลิตร) ใช่ไหม?") instead of answering for it. A smoke check types the awkward case.
96. **A privacy promise is checked on the live site, including what the host adds.** The About text said "ไม่ใช้คุกกี้"; the app sets none, but Cloudflare's bot detection on the `autobahn.bot` zone sets `cf_clearance` (#93). After a change that touches privacy, and before a launch, load the live site in a fresh headless context and read `context.cookies()` and the third-party scripts it loaded; make the text true at once, and report a host or zone setting to the owner rather than changing it.
97. **A tool opened from another says how to get back.** With eleven tools that open each other (the home check offers water and the bag, the plan the card and "I'm safe", the list all of them), the phone's Back closed everything and the person lost their place (F141). Each `open…(from)` marks its opener, and the sheet it opens starts with "‹ " and the opener's name when the opener is a tool or the list (`BACK` in `kit.js`). The phone's Back still closes the sheet ([#94](KNOWN_ISSUES.md#94-leftovers-of-visitor-audit-4)); rule 77 says what a screen that owns a history entry must do.
98. **Live data is read the way the sister project reads it, and the position never leaves the phone.** Before any live feed goes into the app ([ROADMAP N28](ROADMAP.md#n28-home-today-and-home-risk-proposed), [docs/08](08-open-data.md)):
    - **Evidence:** every endpoint, field and number in the docs comes from a live call written down with its date and the host it was made from, or is marked **[unverified]**; an endpoint that failed is listed as refuted so nobody tries it again. A source that refused the research host is "⛔ from there", not "down".
    - **Fetch politely:** an honest User-Agent that names the site; never faster than the source updates (a job every 1–3 hours, not on each visit); a 429 is an answer, so back off; no bot-challenge solving, browser spoofing or geo-block evasion.
    - **Time:** store UTC and write down each source's own rule (GISTDA's "Z" is Bangkok time, HII's times have no offset, RID and DWR use Buddhist years, a rain day runs 07:00–07:00); a unit test with a saved response holds it.
    - **Never mix agencies:** each reading is judged against its own agency's levels (BMA and HII differ by 0.3–0.6 m at the same place); a model (Open-Meteo, CAMS, GloFAS, JRC) is labelled as a model, never shown as a measurement.
    - **The position stays on the phone:** the phone downloads a list (national, provincial or by subdistrict code) and picks what is near it; a source that answers only to a point is used at build time on subdistrict centres or not at all. A subdistrict code chosen by the reader stays in `localStorage` and never goes into an event, a URL or a request (rule 56).
    - **Keys** live on the server that runs the job and go in headers, never in a URL or the browser.
    - **On screen:** each line names its source and time, says when it is old, and still leads to the manual tool (dust today, heat today); "no record" is never shown as "safe"; the app points to DDPM's Cell Broadcast and LINE ALERT for warnings and never presents itself as one.
99. **A server publishes files; it doesn't know people.** When the app gets a server ([docs/09](09-server.md), [ROADMAP N29](ROADMAP.md#n29-a-server-that-publishes-files-proposed)):
    - **It writes files** (by province, by subdistrict code) to static hosting with a long cache and no cookies; the phone matches its own place. It keeps no accounts, positions, push subscriptions or LINE ids, and no request logs beyond what the host needs, until a Thai lawyer has read the plan ([KNOWN_ISSUES #96](KNOWN_ISSUES.md#96-the-pdpa-and-the-traffic-data-rule-apply-once-a-server-keeps-anything-about-a-person)); then a notice with the retention period, consent on a tap, deletion in code.
    - **The app never waits for it:** every tool works without the live files, and a missing or old file shows its age and the manual tool.
    - **Use the simplest key a source gives:** province codes for TMD's warnings (their polygons are the provinces), subdistrict codes where a source has them; measure a precise method against the simple one before building it.
    - **Every row carries its own observed time**; drop rows older than about three times their cadence.
    - **TLS is repaired, never switched off:** add the public intermediates from the CA's own site (Air4Thai's missing Let's Encrypt YR1), verify to the system's roots, alert on failure; and tell the agency.
    - **Keys go in headers and never reach a phone;** strip a key a source echoes back (GISTDA's `links`).
    - **Detect change by content,** hashed after normalising (GISTDA recomputes on every call), when a source sends no validators; ask for gzip.
    - **Name each failure by kind** (DNS, TLS, bot check, timeout from abroad, gone) in the health record; never solve a bot check.
    - **Measure CPU in a fresh process** before choosing a platform: a cycle cost 84–109 ms, so 10 ms platforms are out.

## 9. Working with AI assistants

- **Give the assistant the context first.** [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md) is the entry point: read order, commands, definition of done and sandbox notes. `CLAUDE.md` imports it for Claude Code.
- **Pick work from [ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md),** and update the task's status, [CHANGELOG.md](../CHANGELOG.md) and [KNOWN_ISSUES.md](KNOWN_ISSUES.md) in the same commit.
- **Record new lessons** in [LESSONS_LEARNED.md](LESSONS_LEARNED.md). Turn each into a rule here when it should never happen again.
- **Don't let it make up safety facts.** Ask for the source first. Treat every claim without a source in `sources.json` as `unverified`.
- **Ask for real test output.** The build, `npm test` and, for UI work, `npm run smoke` should be run and their output reported, not summarised from memory.
- **Keep changes small.** One change per commit, written in the style of the code around it, without unrelated rewrites.

## 10. Adding a hazard mode

The registry (`config.json → modes`, defaults in `public/js/modes.js`) makes a new hazard mostly data. Dust and fire (v1.5.0) and the power cut (v1.7.0) were added this way. The build fails on anything missing.

0. **Add a guide first** (v1.9.0, rules 61–62). A new hazard starts as a "what to do" guide with no game: research, 10 sourced lines by phase in `checklists.json` (with optional `phaseNames` when "ก่อนเกิด / ขณะเกิด / หลังเกิด" don't fit the hazard, as for heat, v1.9.1; the build checks them), `config.json → guides` (hazard, icon, and `prop`, the gecko's accessory on the guide's picture, one it already has: `hood`, `torch`, `mask`, `firehat`, `helmet` or `sunhat`, v1.9.2) and `hazards.<hazard>` (slug, `tools`), the strings (`guide<Id>`, `og`/`ogEn.checklistTitle<Id>`, `seo.<lang>.hazard<H><Nav|Title|Desc|H1|Intro>`), a card theme in `cards.js → THEMES`, the pictures, and a line in [04-launch-kit](04-launch-kit.md#when-something-happens-the-5-minute-playbook). The build then writes `/<slug>` (guide first), `/checklist/<id>`, the sitemap, `llms.txt` and `/posts` entries. Look at the new Thai lines at 320 and 360 px, in the guide sheet and on the page, and add any word that breaks to `config.json → keepTogether` (rule 68). That alone is a release; a game mode is a second step, and its lessons may teach only what the guide's sources back. The steps below are for that second step (or for a game mode of an existing guide's hazard, whose guide then becomes the mode's checklist: keep the id, so shared `/checklist/<id>` links keep working).
1. **Research first.** At least one Thai agency source per correct action (see §2.1), cross-checked with an international one where possible. Where only an international one exists, flag the lesson ⚠️ (rule 55). Record what the sources disagree on and leave it out. If a post or infographic started the idea, check each of its claims at an agency; it is not a source itself.
2. **Registry entry** in `config.json → modes`: `id` (a–z), a new one-letter `code` (never reuse one; `p`, `r`, `d`, `f` and `s` are taken, `h` is kept for heat), `hazard`, `threat` (`water`, `smoke`, `haze`, `dark` or `null`), `prop` (in `mascot.js`), `emoji`, optional `icon`, `months` (Bangkok months it is in season; empty for a year-round mode that is never the default, like the power cut), `topic`, optional `link` (https). Mirror it in `modes.js` `DEFAULTS`.
3. **Round settings:** `round.<id>.seconds` and the threat's keyframes (`water` + `floorWetY`, `smoke` + `lowY`, `haze`, or `dark` + `hours`). A `dark` mode needs `round.<id>.hours` (the story's length), and its items may use `lostAfter: <hours>` (between 0 and `hours`) to go bad at that hour; give at least one item a sourced time, or the build warns, and every house of the mode will include one. `lostAfter` in a mode whose threat isn't `dark` stops the build. Check the timings with `npm run probe`.
4. **Strings** in `th` and `en` (the English in British English, rule 58): `mode`, `mode<Mode>Desc`, `hero`, `challenge`, `howto`, `howtoTitle`, `geckoStart`, `result`, `shareText`, `checklistTab`, `rank` (3), and the endings `cheerPerfect`, `cheerHigh`, `cheerMid`, `cheerLow` (a unit test checks them), each as `<base><Mode>`. Optional overrides that fall back to the shared key: `floodedToast`, `geckoFlood`, `geckoWet`, `stFlooded`, `forecastShort`, `forecastLong`, `forecastIcon`. Plus `og`/`ogEn` `challengeTitle`, `challengeDesc`, `challengeLine`, `checklistTitle`, and `seo.th/en.section<Mode>`.
5. **A new hazard needs a page.** If the mode brings a new `hazard` (not a second flood mode), add `config.json → hazards.<hazard>.slug`: lowercase letters, digits or "-", unique, and not a site path (`c`, `checklist`, `en`, `learn`, `api`, `js` and the other reserved names in `tools/build.mjs`) or an existing file in `public/`. Pick it with care, because it can never change once published. Add the `seo` strings `hazard<H>Nav`, `hazard<H>Title`, `hazard<H>Desc`, `hazard<H>H1` and `hazard<H>Intro` in `th` and `en` (`<H>` is the hazard id with a capital, for example `hazardHeatH1`). The build then writes `/<slug>` and `/en/<slug>` and adds them to the sitemap, `llms.txt` and the "By hazard" row; it stops if the slug or a string is missing. Keep the page text free of advice, numbers and agency names; the lessons carry those. A hazard can also link the home check and the plan card (`hazards.<hazard>.tools: ["kit", "plan"]`, with `seo.<lang>.toolKit` and `toolPlan`), as `/ready` and, since v1.8.1, `/flood` do. Since v1.8.1 the same list puts the 🎒 home-check button on the result screen of the hazard's modes, so give a hazard `kit` only where a household stock fits (power cut, floods), not where it doesn't (dust, fire). Since v1.10.0 the list may also hold the calculators, `fridge` (where a power cut follows: `outage`, `storm`) and `air` (`dust`), with `seo.<lang>.toolFridge` and `toolAir`; they appear on the page, in the guide's next steps and on the result screen of the hazard's modes. A new calculator follows rules 78–81 and gets its own id in `TOOL_IDS` (`tools/build.mjs`) and `TOOLS` (`main.js`), a page, a preview and a smoke check.
6. **Content:** 10–13 items across the three drawn rooms (at least one per room), 5 decoys, a 10-item checklist with sprites, a `topic_<id>` string pair and the topic in `config.topics`.
7. **Start button:** add a static `data-mode` button to `index.html` (the JS rebuilds the grid from the registry; the static one avoids a jump on first paint). Card colours in `app.css` (`.mode-btn.mode-<id>`, `.is-<id>` layers, and hide `.mud` and `.sky-rain` for a non-flood mode). The small cards share one row of up to four (`--cols` in `main.js`, and the static `--cols` in `index.html`); with five modes all four small names fit on one line at 320 px in Thai. A sixth mode needs a new layout, checked at 320 px in both languages.
8. **Card theme** in `cards.js → THEMES`, then `SITE_URL=… npm run images` and commit only the new images.
9. **Rewrite what describes the whole game** (rule 59): the tagline in `strings.json` (the build writes it into `index.html`, rule 65), `index.html`'s `<title>` and meta description, the manifest's description, the default preview texts and image, the About text, `/learn`'s title, heading, description and intro, `seo.en.llmsSummary`, and `package.json → description`, in Thai and English. Search for the old hazard list.
10. **Check:** build, `npm test`, `npm run probe`, `npm run smoke`, a visitor pass at 320 px and on a tablet in both languages (including `/?mode=<id>` and the new hazard page), and compare every other mode's houses before and after over a year (they must not change). If the mode has `months`, also run the smoke test with `SMOKE_DATE` set to a day in its season. If its sprites sit on a slot near the room arrows, look at the arrow check (rule 54).
11. **Carousel and posts:** add `carousel.json → sets.<hazard>` (at most 8 playable lessons of the hazard) with `cover<H>Title`, `cover<H>Sub` and `caption<H>` in both languages, render with `npm run images -- --only share/carousel`, and add a post to [04-launch-kit.md](04-launch-kit.md).
