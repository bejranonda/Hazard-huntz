10 things for a fire-safe home

  1. Power strips with the TIS 2432 mark; never chain them
  2. Cracked, hot or burning-smelling cord? Stop and unplug
  3. Don't charge phones on beds or sofas
  4. Stay with the pan; turn the stove off if you leave
  5. After cooking, close the cylinder valve and the stove
  6. Keep flammables away from the stove and heat
  7. Keep an extinguisher in sight, ready to grab
  8. No open burning, especially on windy days
  9. In a fire, touch the door first; if hot, keep it shut
  10. Wet cloth over nose and mouth, crawl low, get out, call 199

House fire

Smoke under the door ควันลอดใต้ประตู

Front yard · 🔥 Fire safety

Do this: Touch the door first; if it's hot, keep it shut and find another way

In a fire, touch the door first. Hot: keep it shut. Cool: open slowly.

Why: DDPM says don't open a door straight away: feel the door or handle first. If it's hot, don't open it; if not, open it slowly.

Sources: Department of Disaster Prevention and Mitigation (DDPM) · Department of Disaster Prevention and Mitigation (DDPM) (2020)

Dry leaf pile on a windy day กองขยะใบไม้แห้ง วันลมแรง

Front yard · 🔥 Fire safety

Do this: Don't burn, especially in wind; bag it for the bin

Don't burn rubbish or leaves in the open, especially on windy days.

Why: DDPM says that in dry, windy weather people should not burn rubbish or weeds in the open or in strong wind, and never leave a fire unattended.

Sources: Department of Disaster Prevention and Mitigation (DDPM) (2025)

Cigarette end still smoking ก้นบุหรี่ยังมีควัน

Front yard · 🔥 Fire safety

Do this: Put it out completely before binning it

Put cigarettes out fully before binning; don't smoke near things that burn.

Why: DDPM says put cigarette ends out completely before throwing them away, and avoid smoking near flammable material.

Sources: Department of Disaster Prevention and Mitigation (DDPM) (2025)

Power strips plugged into each other ปลั๊กพ่วงต่อกันเป็นทอดๆ

Living room · 🔥 Fire safety

Do this: Unchain them, and don't run high-power appliances together

Never chain power strips, and don't run power-hungry appliances together.

Why: MEA says use strips with the TIS 2432-2555 mark, never chain them, and don't run microwaves, electric pans or irons together on one. DDPM also says don't load one strip with many appliances.

Sources: Metropolitan Electricity Authority (MEA) (2026) · Department of Disaster Prevention and Mitigation (DDPM) (2025)

Lamp cord running hot สายไฟโคมไฟร้อนผิดปกติ

Living room · 🔥 Fire safety

Do this: Stop using it, unplug it, then repair or replace it

Cord cracked, hot or smelling of burning? Stop, unplug, repair or replace.

Why: DDPM says repair or replace damaged appliances and cracked or burning-smelling cords at once. MEA says stop using and cut the power when a cord is damaged or unusually hot.

Sources: Department of Disaster Prevention and Mitigation (DDPM) (2025) · Metropolitan Electricity Authority (MEA) (2026)

Phone charging on the sofa มือถือชาร์จอยู่บนโซฟา

Living room · 🔥 Fire safety

Do this: Move it to a table; never charge on a sofa or bed

Don't charge phones on beds or sofas. Use a table.

Why: Among DDPM's fire-prevention tips for the dry season: don't charge smartphones on a bed or sofa.

Sources: Department of Disaster Prevention and Mitigation (DDPM) (2025)

Smoke alarm chirping เครื่องจับควันส่งเสียงปี๊บ

Living room · 🔥 Fire safety

Do this: Change the battery, press test, and test it monthly

Test smoke alarms every month, and change a low battery right away.

Why: The US Fire Administration says test smoke alarms monthly and change batteries at least yearly; when one sounds you may have under 2 minutes to get out. No Thai agency guidance on this was found yet.

Sources: U.S. Fire Administration (FEMA) (2026) · ⚠️ being reviewed with the agency

This towel ผ้าขนหนูผืนนี้

Living room · 🔥 Fire safety

Do this: Wet it, cover nose and mouth, and crawl low to get out

In a fire: wet cloth over nose and mouth, crawl low, get out.

Why: DDPM's fire survival steps: cover nose and mouth with a (wet) cloth against smoke, and crawl low because clean air is near the floor. Don't retreat into the bathroom.

Sources: Department of Disaster Prevention and Mitigation (DDPM) · Department of Disaster Prevention and Mitigation (DDPM) (2020)

Pan of oil on the stove กระทะน้ำมันบนเตา

Kitchen · 🔥 Fire safety

Do this: Turn the stove off before leaving; stay with the pan

Stay with the pan. Turn the stove off before you leave the kitchen.

Why: DDPM says never leave flames or sparks unattended. The US Fire Administration says cooking is the leading cause of home fires: stand by your pan and turn the burner off if you leave.

Sources: Department of Disaster Prevention and Mitigation (DDPM) (2025) · U.S. Fire Administration (FEMA) (2026)

Gas cylinder after cooking ถังแก๊สหลังทำอาหารเสร็จ

Kitchen · 🔥 Fire safety

Do this: Close the cylinder valve and the stove fully

After cooking, always close the cylinder valve and the stove.

Why: DDPM says check the stove and LPG cylinder are in good order and close the cylinder valve after every use.

Sources: Department of Disaster Prevention and Mitigation (DDPM) (2020) · Department of Disaster Prevention and Mitigation (DDPM) (2025)

Sprays and thinner by the stove สเปรย์และทินเนอร์ข้างเตา

Kitchen · 🔥 Fire safety

Do this: Move them away from the stove and any heat

Keep flammables like sprays and thinner away from the stove and heat.

Why: DDPM says store flammable chemicals away from heat. The US Fire Administration also says keep anything that can burn away from the stovetop.

Sources: Department of Disaster Prevention and Mitigation (DDPM) (2025) · U.S. Fire Administration (FEMA) (2026)

Extinguisher in the cupboard ถังดับเพลิงในตู้

Kitchen · 🔥 Fire safety

Do this: Move it somewhere visible you can grab at once

Keep the extinguisher where you can see it and grab it fast.

Why: DDPM says install an extinguisher where it can be used easily, and use it on a fire while it is still small.

Sources: Department of Disaster Prevention and Mitigation (DDPM) (2025)

Phone numbers on the fridge โน้ตเบอร์โทรข้างตู้เย็น

Kitchen · 🔥 Fire safety

Do this: Call 199 for the fire service

A fire you can't put out: get out and call 199 right away.

Why: DDPM says in a serious fire, call the fire service on 199 at once and get ready to leave.

Sources: Department of Disaster Prevention and Mitigation (DDPM) · Department of Disaster Prevention and Mitigation (DDPM) (2020)

Thai emergency helplines

  • 1784 DDPM disaster hotline: Report disasters, ask for help, 24 h
  • 199 Fire: A fire you can't put out: get out, then call the fire service
  • 1669 Medical emergency (NIEMS): Ambulance, 24 h
  • 1555 Bangkok (BMA): Report problems and get help in Bangkok
  • 1130 MEA electricity: Bangkok, Nonthaburi, Samut Prakan
  • 1129 PEA electricity: The other 74 provinces
  • 1323 Mental health hotline: Stressed or worn out after the flood? Free, 24 h
