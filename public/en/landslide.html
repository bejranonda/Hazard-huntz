Flash floods and landslides: how to prepare, and the signs that mean go

BeforeDuringAfter

☎️ Free calls17841991669

Before

  1. Check if your home is by a slope or stream at risk
    Sources: Department of Mineral Resources (DMR) (2022) · Department of Water Resources (DWR) · ⚠️ being reviewed with the agency
  2. Follow warnings on the THA DISASTER ALERT app
    Sources: Department of Disaster Prevention and Mitigation (DDPM) (2026) · Department of Mineral Resources (DMR) · Department of Water Resources (DWR)
  3. Know your village's warning signal and agreed safe spot
    Sources: Department of Mineral Resources (DMR) (2022)
  4. Plan your evacuation route to the safe spot in advance
    Sources: Department of Water Resources (DWR)
  5. Plan how you'll help children, older and bedridden people
    Sources: Department of Disaster Prevention and Mitigation (DDPM) · Department of Disaster Prevention and Mitigation (DDPM) (2025)

During

  1. Heavy rain that won't stop? Get ready to evacuate
    Sources: Department of Mineral Resources (DMR) (2024) · Department of Mineral Resources (DMR) (2025)
  2. Stream turns muddy or a loud odd noise? Leave at once
    Sources: Department of Mineral Resources (DMR) · Department of Mineral Resources (DMR) (2022) · Department of Mineral Resources (DMR) (2025)
  3. Ground starting to crack? Move to a safe place at once
    Sources: Department of Mineral Resources (DMR) · Ready.gov (U.S. FEMA)
  4. Never cross a road with water or mud flowing over it
    Sources: Ready.gov (U.S. FEMA) · ⚠️ being reviewed with the agency

After

  1. Keep away from the slide until officials say it's safe
    Sources: Ready.gov (U.S. FEMA) · Department of Disaster Prevention and Mitigation (DDPM) (2026) · ⚠️ being reviewed with the agency

Ten short steps for before, during and after a flash flood or mudslide, each with its source; the ones still being reviewed are marked. There is no game on this yet: read it, then send it to family as a picture.

Most of this advice comes from Thai government agencies, and the helplines below are Thai numbers (199 is the Thai fire service). Most of it applies anywhere, but outside Thailand, follow your local authorities and call your local emergency number.

Thai emergency helplines

  • 1784 DDPM disaster hotline: Report disasters, ask for help, 24 h
  • 199 Fire: A fire you can't put out: get out, then call the fire service
  • 1669 Medical emergency (NIEMS): Ambulance, 24 h
  • 1555 Bangkok (BMA): Report problems and get help in Bangkok
  • 1130 MEA electricity: Bangkok, Nonthaburi, Samut Prakan
  • 1129 PEA electricity: The other 74 provinces
  • 1323 Mental health hotline: Stressed or worn out after the flood? Free, 24 h
