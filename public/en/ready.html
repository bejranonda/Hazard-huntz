10 things for a 3-day power cut

  1. Drinking water: 3 litres per person per day, for 3 days
  2. Dry and canned food for 3 days; eat it and restock
  3. A manual can opener, kept with the food
  4. A torch for everyone, with spare batteries
  5. A battery radio for official news
  6. Phones and power banks kept charged
  7. 7 days of daily medicine; a plan for cold medicine
  8. Keep the fridge shut: about 4 hours for fresh food
  9. Unplug appliances against surges
  10. Generators outdoors only; torches, not candles

Power cuts and home stock

A generator under the window เครื่องปั่นไฟชิดหน้าต่าง

Front of the house · ⚡ Power & gas

Do this: Move it into the open, away from doors and windows

Run a generator only outdoors in the open, away from doors and windows.

Why: Thailand's Department of Disease Control warns against running generators in enclosed spaces because of toxic gas, and Ready.gov says to run them outdoors at least 20 feet (about 6 m) from windows, doors and attached garages. Carbon monoxide has no colour or smell.

Sources: Department of Disease Control (DDC) (2025) · Ready.gov (U.S. FEMA) (2026)

Grandma's medicine that must stay cold ยาที่ต้องแช่เย็นของคุณยาย

Living room · 🦠 Health after floods

Do this: Plan ahead for keeping it cold in a power cut

Medicine that must stay cold, or a medical device on power? Plan ahead.

Why: Ready.gov (US FEMA) says to have alternate plans for refrigerating medicines and for power-dependent medical devices. If a medicine was not kept cold during a power cut, the US FDA says to check its label or ask a pharmacist whether it can still be used; a medicine needed to sustain life, such as insulin, may be used until a new supply is found.

Sources: Ready.gov (U.S. FEMA) (2026) · U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) (2026) · ⚠️ being reviewed with the agency

A candle left burning เทียนที่จุดทิ้งไว้

Living room · 🔥 Fire safety

Do this: Blow it out and use a torch

In a power cut use torches, not candles. Never leave a candle burning.

Why: The US Fire Administration says to use torches, not candles, in a power cut, to blow candles out when you leave a room or go to bed, and to keep them at least 12 inches from anything that burns.

Sources: U.S. Fire Administration (FEMA) (2023) · Ready.gov (U.S. FEMA) (2026) · ⚠️ being reviewed with the agency

A torch with flat batteries ไฟฉายถ่านหมด

Living room · 🎒 Plans & go-bag

Do this: Keep spare batteries with it, and a torch for everyone

Keep spare batteries with the torch, and a torch for everyone.

Why: DDPM lists a torch with spare batteries among the 6 things every emergency kit needs, and Ready.gov says to have a torch for every household member in a power cut.

Sources: Department of Disaster Prevention and Mitigation (DDPM) · Ready.gov (U.S. FEMA) (2026)

A battery radio วิทยุใส่ถ่าน

Living room · 🎒 Plans & go-bag

Do this: Turn on a battery radio for official news

Keep a battery radio: official news still gets through when the power is out.

Why: The Thai government's 72-hour kit checklist (PRD) lists a torch, a radio, a phone and a power bank, and Ready.gov lists a battery-powered or hand-crank radio.

Sources: Government Public Relations Department (PRD) (2026) · Ready.gov (U.S. FEMA) (2026) · ⚠️ being reviewed with the agency

A power bank down to one bar พาวเวอร์แบงก์ที่แบตเหลือขีดเดียว

Living room · 🎒 Plans & go-bag

Do this: Charge the power bank and phones fully now

Charge phones and power banks fully before the power goes.

Why: DDPM lists chargers and a power bank among the things to prepare, and Ready.gov suggests a back-up power source such as a power bank.

Sources: Department of Disaster Prevention and Mitigation (DDPM) (2025) · Ready.gov (U.S. FEMA) (2026)

TV and computer still plugged in ปลั๊กทีวีกับคอมที่เสียบค้าง

Living room · ⚡ Power & gas

Do this: Unplug them in case of a surge when power returns

Power cut? Unplug appliances and electronics against a surge when it returns.

Why: Ready.gov (US FEMA): disconnect appliances and electronics to avoid damage from electrical surges, because power may return with momentary surges or spikes.

Sources: Ready.gov (U.S. FEMA) (2026) · ⚠️ being reviewed with the agency

The fridge in a power cut ตู้เย็นตอนไฟดับ

Kitchen · 💧 Water & food

Do this: Keep the door shut; open it only when you must

Power cut? Keep the fridge shut: about 4 hours safe. Then bin meat, milk, eggs.

Why: The US CDC says a closed fridge keeps food safe for up to 4 hours. After 4 hours without power, throw out chilled food such as meat, fish, eggs, milk and leftovers. That's why, in this game, the fridge is the first thing lost, at the 4-hour mark.

Sources: U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) (2025) · Ready.gov (U.S. FEMA) (2026) · ⚠️ being reviewed with the agency

Minced pork after a night without power หมูสับหลังไฟดับทั้งคืน

Kitchen · 💧 Water & food

Do this: Throw it out without tasting it

Out over 4 hours? Bin chilled meat, fish, eggs and milk. Never taste to check.

Why: The US CDC: never taste food to find out if it is safe. After 4 hours without power, throw out chilled perishables such as meat, fish, eggs and milk, and when in doubt, throw it out.

Sources: U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) (2025) · ⚠️ being reviewed with the agency

A full freezer ช่องแช่แข็งที่แน่นเต็ม

Kitchen · 💧 Water & food

Do this: Keep it shut: a full freezer stays frozen about 2 days

Kept shut, a full freezer holds about 48 hours, a half-full one about 24.

Why: The US CDC: with the door closed, frozen food lasts about 48 hours in a full freezer and about 24 in a half-full one. The game's power cut lasts 12 hours, so the freezer isn't lost.

Sources: U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) (2025) · ⚠️ being reviewed with the agency

A shelf full of cans อาหารกระป๋องเต็มตู้

Kitchen · 💧 Water & food

Do this: Keep a manual can opener with the food

Stocking cans? Keep a manual can opener with them.

Why: Ready.gov (US FEMA) lists a manual can opener in its emergency kit, next to the non-perishable food.

Sources: Ready.gov (U.S. FEMA) (2026) · ⚠️ being reviewed with the agency

Stored drinking water น้ำดื่มสำรอง

Kitchen · 💧 Water & food

Do this: Store at least 3 litres per person per day

Store at least 3 litres of drinking water per person per day, for 3 days.

Why: The Thai government's 72-hour kit checklist (PRD) says at least 3 litres of drinking water per person per day, and DDPM advises food and water for at least 3 days.

Sources: Government Public Relations Department (PRD) (2026) · Department of Disaster Prevention and Mitigation (DDPM) (2025) · ⚠️ being reviewed with the agency

The food store ตู้ของตุน

Kitchen · 💧 Water & food

Do this: Eat it as usual, restock, replace what's expired

Buy a bit extra of what you eat, use it, restock, and watch the dates.

Why: The Thai Department of Health advises dry and canned food for at least 3–5 days in sealed containers. Tokyo's 'everyday stockpiling' means keeping a little extra of what you normally use and using it; Ready.gov says to replace expired items.

Sources: Department of Health (DOH) (2025) · Tokyo Metropolitan Government · Ready.gov (U.S. FEMA) (2026) · ⚠️ being reviewed with the agency
