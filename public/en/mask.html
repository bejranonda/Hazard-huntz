When, which kind, and the sources

  1. 🌫️ PM2.5 dust, or smoke from burning
    When (by the dust colour): Blue: not yet · Green: not yet · Yellow: the people at risk · Orange: everyone, when going out · Red: everyone, when going out
    At risk: A child aged 2 to 5, Someone over 60, Someone pregnant, Someone with heart or lung disease
    Choose an N95 first, in a size that fits your face
    An N95, or a 3-layer surgical mask instead
    A 3-layer surgical mask that fits the child’s face ⚠️
    Follow your doctor’s advice (Someone with heart or lung disease)
    Don’t exercise in an N95: exercise indoors instead
    If it makes breathing hard, or you get a tight chest, a headache or feel sick, take it off and go indoors
    👶 Children under 2 shouldn’t wear any kind of mask: keep them in a room that keeps the dust out, and away from dusty places ⚠️
    Sources: Pollution Control Department (PCD) (2023) · Pollution Control Department (PCD) (2023) · Department of Health (DOH) (2025) · Department of Disease Control (DDC) (2025) · Department of Health (DOH) (2021) · Department of Health (DOH) (2026) · Public Health Agency of Canada (2026) · ⚠️ being reviewed with the agency
  2. 🧹 Cleaning up after a flood: Mould
    An N95, rubber gloves, rubber boots and goggles
    Open doors and windows; no fan or air-con while you wipe the mould
    Don’t wipe it dry: use a cloth dipped in water and detergent
    Allergies, asthma or lung disease: take extra care with mould
    Keep children and pets out until the house has been made safe
    Sources: Department of Disease Control (DDC) (2025) · Department of Health (DOH) (2022)
  3. 🧹 Cleaning up after a flood: Dust from dried mud
    Dust from dried mud: a surgical mask
    Use a vacuum cleaner, not a broom, so the dust doesn’t fly
    Allergies, asthma or lung disease: take extra care with mould
    Keep children and pets out until the house has been made safe
    Sources: Department of Disease Control (DDC) (2025)
  4. 🧹 Cleaning up after a flood: Taking down broken ceiling boards or tiles
    Not sure whether it has asbestos? Take it down slowly, without breaking it
    Wear an N95 or better
    Allergies, asthma or lung disease: take extra care with mould
    Keep children and pets out until the house has been made safe
    Sources: Department of Disease Control (DDC) (2025)
  5. 🤧 A cough or fever, or a crowded place: Wear a surgical mask
    Ill, coughing or feverish? Wear a surgical mask every time you must go out, and see a doctor
    Crowded places, such as hospitals and schools: a surgical mask
    When ill, keep away from others; and keep your distance from someone who is ill
    Cover coughs and sneezes; wash your hands with soap and water often
    Sources: Department of Disease Control (DDC) (2026)
  6. 🏭 A factory or warehouse fire nearby: Get away: a mask won’t protect you
    Do what officials say: if told to leave, go at once, upwind, and keep out of the smoke
    Surgical masks and N95s can’t filter chemical fumes ⚠️
    Dizzy or sick after breathing it? Get to fresh air at once and call 1669
    Someone trapped? Don’t go back in: tell the officials
    After the fire, don’t go near or touch the chemicals until officials say so
    Sources: Department of Health (DOH) (2021) · Chulalongkorn University (2021) · Public Health Agency of Canada (2026) · ⚠️ being reviewed with the agency
  7. 🔥 A fire in your home: Get out of the house
    Cover your nose and mouth with cloth, keep low and crawl out
    Feel a door before opening it: if it’s hot, don’t
    Never use a lift
    A serious fire: call 199
    Sources: Department of Disaster Prevention and Mitigation (DDPM)
  8. 😷 Wearing, changing and buying one, and what it can't do
    Over the nose and under the chin, snug
    Press the wire onto the bridge of your nose
    Cup it with both hands and breathe out hard: no air at the edges means a good fit
    Change it when it’s loose, harder to breathe through, torn, dirty or wet
    Don’t share it, and don’t wash it to wear again
    To throw it away: a sealed bag, a bin with a lid, then wash your hands
    A Thai label saying the type, the maker and how to use it
    An N95: the box names a standard such as NIOSH, EN149 or GB2626
    A surgical mask: at least 3 layers, with a filter in the middle
    A cloth mask stops little fine dust: not enough on high-dust days
    A dust mask doesn’t stop gases or chemical fumes ⚠️
    Sources: Department of Health (DOH) (2025) · Department of Health (DOH) (2022) · Ramathibodi Hospital (Mahidol University) (2015) · Office of the Consumer Protection Board (OCPB) (2026) · Department of Disease Control (DDC) (2025) · Public Health Agency of Canada (2026) · Chulalongkorn University (2021) · ⚠️ being reviewed with the agency
