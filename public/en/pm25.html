10 things to do on PM2.5 days

  1. Check the dust level in Air4Thai before going out
  2. High-dust day: shut doors and windows, seal the gaps
  3. Run a purifier; check and change the filter every 6 months
  4. Wipe with a damp cloth instead of sweeping
  5. Wet-wipe fans, air-cons, window screens and bedding
  6. Going out? A snug N95 over the nose and under the chin
  7. Dust over the limit? Exercise indoors, not outside
  8. Kids, elders, pregnant and ill family stay in; keep medicines ready
  9. Frequent cough, hard breathing or tight chest: see a doctor
  10. No open burning of rubbish or leaves; less charcoal grilling

PM2.5 dust and smoke

Thin cloth mask หน้ากากผ้าบางๆ

Front yard · 😷 Dust and smoke

Do this: Wear an N95 that fits snugly, over the nose and under the chin

Going out? Wear an N95 that fits snugly over the nose and under the chin.

Why: The Thai Department of Health says wear an N95 when you must go out; it must fit snugly over the nose and under the chin, and be replaced when loose, torn or dirty. WHO also advises a well-fitting N95/FFP2.

Sources: Department of Health (DOH) (2026) · Department of Health (DOH) (2022) · World Health Organization (WHO), Regional Office for Europe (2023)

Running shoes รองเท้าวิ่ง

Front yard · 😷 Dust and smoke

Do this: Skip the outdoor run and exercise indoors

When dust is over the limit, skip outdoor exercise and work out indoors.

Why: The Thai Department of Health says that when PM2.5 is over the standard, avoid outdoor activity and exercise, and exercise indoors instead.

Sources: Department of Health (DOH) (2021) · Department of Health (DOH) (2022)

The kids' ball ลูกบอลของหลาน

Front yard · 😷 Dust and smoke

Do this: Play indoors instead; children are at higher risk

Kids, elders, pregnant women and the ill are at risk. Keep them in on red days.

Why: The Thai Department of Health names children, pregnant women, older people and those with chronic illness as at risk; on red days they should stay in. WHO names the same groups.

Sources: Department of Health (DOH) (2022) · Department of Health (DOH) (2026) · World Health Organization (WHO), Regional Office for Europe (2023)

Dry leaves and a box of matches กองใบไม้แห้งกับไม้ขีด

Front yard · 😷 Dust and smoke

Do this: Don't burn it; bag it up with the rubbish

Don't burn rubbish or leaves in the open; it adds PM2.5 for the whole street.

Why: The Thai Department of Health asks everyone to help cut PM2.5: no open burning, fewer black-smoke diesels, and planting trees.

Sources: Department of Health (DOH) (2021)

Wide-open window หน้าต่างเปิดอ้า

Living room · 😷 Dust and smoke

Do this: Shut it tight and seal the gaps round the frame

On high-dust days, shut doors and windows and seal the gaps to keep dust out.

Why: The Thai Department of Health says to shut doors and windows fully and seal gaps round them. WHO also advises staying in with windows shut when smoke is heavy.

Sources: Department of Health (DOH) (2026) · Department of Health (DOH) (2021) · World Health Organization (WHO), Regional Office for Europe (2023)

Broom on a dusty floor ไม้กวาดกับพื้นฝุ่นเขรอะ

Living room · 😷 Dust and smoke

Do this: Wipe with a damp cloth instead of sweeping

Wipe with a damp cloth instead of sweeping, so the dust stays down.

Why: The Thai Department of Health says to clean with a damp cloth rather than a broom, so dust is not stirred up.

Sources: Department of Health (DOH) (2026) · Department of Health (DOH) (2021)

Air purifier เครื่องฟอกอากาศ

Living room · 😷 Dust and smoke

Do this: Check the filter and replace it on schedule

Check the purifier filter; health officials say replace it every 6 months.

Why: The Thai Department of Health suggests a purifier or fresh-air system for a dust-free room, and checking and replacing filters every 6 months.

Sources: Department of Health (DOH) (2026)

Grandpa's inhaler ยาพ่นของคุณตา

Living room · 🦠 Health after floods

Do this: Get his medicine and kit ready, as his doctor says

Long-term illness? Keep medicine and kit ready, as your doctor advises.

Why: The Thai Department of Health says people with a long-term illness should keep medicine and equipment ready and follow their doctor. On red days, have at least 5 days of medicine.

Sources: Department of Health (DOH) (2021) · Department of Health (DOH) (2022)

Grandma keeps coughing คุณยายไอไม่หยุด

Living room · 🦠 Health after floods

Do this: Take her to a doctor soon

Coughing, short of breath or chest tightness in heavy dust? See a doctor soon.

Why: The Thai Department of Health says watch yourself and your family; frequent coughing, trouble breathing, a tight or painful chest mean see a doctor soon. WHO says the same.

Sources: Department of Health (DOH) (2021) · World Health Organization (WHO), Regional Office for Europe (2023)

Dusty fan พัดลมฝุ่นจับหนา

Kitchen · 😷 Dust and smoke

Do this: Take the grille off and wipe with a wet cloth

Wipe dust traps like fans, air-cons and screens with a wet cloth.

Why: The Thai Department of Health says to clean dust traps such as air-cons, fans, window screens and bedding with a wet cloth.

Sources: Department of Health (DOH) (2021)

Phone before the market run มือถือก่อนออกไปตลาด

Kitchen · 😷 Dust and smoke

Do this: Check the dust level in an air-quality app and follow its advice

Before going out, check the dust in the Air4Thai app and follow its advice.

Why: The Thai Department of Health says to check air quality before leaving home, on the Pollution Control Department's Air4Thai site or app (or AirBKK), and follow the advice.

Sources: Department of Health (DOH) (2021) · Department of Health (DOH) (2026)

Charcoal grill เตาถ่านปิ้งย่าง

Kitchen · 😷 Dust and smoke

Do this: Skip the charcoal today and cook another way

On high-dust days, skip the charcoal grill to cut smoke in and around the home.

Why: The Thai Department of Health asks people to cut charcoal grilling and open burning. WHO advises reducing indoor pollution such as frying or grilling.

Sources: Department of Health (DOH) (2021) · World Health Organization (WHO), Regional Office for Europe (2023)

  • 1669 Medical emergency (NIEMS): Ambulance, 24 h
  • 1555 Bangkok (BMA): Report problems and get help in Bangkok
  • 1130 MEA electricity: Bangkok, Nonthaburi, Samut Prakan
  • 1129 PEA electricity: The other 74 provinces
  • 1323 Mental health hotline: Stressed or worn out after the flood? Free, 24 h
