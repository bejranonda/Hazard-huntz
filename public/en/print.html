🌊 10 things to do before the flood

  • Seal key documents in a waterproof bag in your emergency bag
  • Water and dry food for at least 3 days
  • At least 7 days of regular medicine, plus first aid
  • Torch, spare batteries, phone and power bank charged
  • Lift appliances, valuables and chemicals up high
  • Breaker off while dry; gas valve shut, cylinder tied up high
  • Sandbag the doors; plug downstairs toilets and drains
  • Move the car to official parking, never onto bridges
  • Get older people, bedridden relatives and pets out early
  • Know your nearest shelter; save 1784 and 1669

🧹 10 checks before going back in

  • Check the structure first: cracks, sinking floors, sagging ceilings
  • Rubber boots and gloves whenever you wade water or mud
  • Don't touch wet sockets, switches or the breaker; call an electrician
  • Don't plug in flooded appliances or start a flooded car
  • Downed line: stay 3–5 m away and call 1130 or 1129
  • Smell gas: no switches or flames, open windows, leave and call
  • Check shoes, cupboards and debris with a stick for snakes
  • Bin flood-touched food and damaged cans; boil tap water
  • Never mix bleach and toilet cleaner; no generators indoors
  • Fever after wading? See a doctor; don't self-medicate

⛰️ Flash floods and landslides: 10 steps

Before

  • Check if your home is by a slope or stream at risk ⚠️
  • Follow warnings on the THA DISASTER ALERT app
  • Know your village's warning signal and agreed safe spot
  • Plan your evacuation route to the safe spot in advance
  • Plan how you'll help children, older and bedridden people

During

  • Heavy rain that won't stop? Get ready to evacuate
  • Stream turns muddy or a loud odd noise? Leave at once
  • Ground starting to crack? Move to a safe place at once
  • Never cross a road with water or mud flowing over it ⚠️

After

  • Keep away from the slide until officials say it's safe ⚠️

⛈️ Storms, wind and lightning: 10 steps

Before

  • Follow Met Department storm warnings and act on them
  • Check the roof, doors and windows are secure
  • Put away anything the wind could blow about
  • Trim weak branches on big trees near the house

During

  • Stay in a sturdy building with doors and windows shut
  • Don't shelter under big trees, billboards or power poles
  • In lightning, keep off rooftops, balconies and open ground
  • In lightning, don't use anything plugged into the mains

After

  • Keep away from fallen lines or poles; call 1130 or 1129
  • After the storm, check electrics and repair any damage ⚠️

🏚️ Earthquake: 10 things to do

Before

  • Fix heavy appliances firmly to the wall or floor
  • Keep heavy things off high shelves and ledges
  • Keep a torch, spare batteries and a first-aid kit at home

During

  • Drop, cover and hold on under a sturdy table
  • Keep away from glass doors and windows
  • By the sea: a quake or a sudden ebb? Go to high ground ⚠️

After

  • Leave by the fire stairs, never the lift
  • Don't rush back in; wait for a check or official word
  • Smell gas? Shut the cylinder valve, open doors and windows
  • Wear closed shoes against broken glass and debris

🔥 10 things for a fire-safe home

  • Power strips with the TIS 2432 mark; never chain them
  • Cracked, hot or burning-smelling cord? Stop and unplug
  • Don't charge phones on beds or sofas
  • Stay with the pan; turn the stove off if you leave
  • After cooking, close the cylinder valve and the stove
  • Keep flammables away from the stove and heat
  • Keep an extinguisher in sight, ready to grab
  • No open burning, especially on windy days
  • In a fire, touch the door first; if hot, keep it shut
  • Wet cloth over nose and mouth, crawl low, get out, call 199

🔦 10 things for a 3-day power cut

  • Drinking water: 3 litres per person per day, for 3 days
  • Dry and tinned food for 3 days; eat it and restock
  • A manual can opener, kept with the food
  • A torch for everyone, with spare batteries
  • A battery radio for official news
  • Phones and power banks kept charged
  • 7 days of daily medicine; a plan for cold medicine
  • Keep the fridge shut: about 4 hours for fresh food
  • Unplug appliances against surges
  • Generators outdoors only; torches, not candles

🌡️ Extreme heat: 10 ways to look after each other

Get ready

  • Look after elders, young children, pregnant women, the ill
  • Follow heat warnings and the heat index
  • Keep water by elders; have them sip before they feel thirsty
  • Set up a cool, airy room for elders to rest in

Very hot days

  • Wear light-coloured, loose clothes that breathe
  • Move outdoor activity to the morning or evening
  • Avoid alcohol on very hot days
  • Never leave a child, or anyone, in a car parked in the sun

Heatstroke

  • Very hot, not sweating, confused? Call 1669 at once
  • Into the shade, loosen clothes, wipe with a cool wet cloth

😷 10 things to do on PM2.5 days

  • Check the dust level in Air4Thai before going out
  • High-dust day: shut doors and windows, seal the gaps
  • Run a purifier; check and change the filter every 6 months
  • Wipe with a damp cloth instead of sweeping
  • Wet-wipe fans, air-cons, window screens and bedding
  • Going out? A snug N95 over the nose and under the chin
  • Dust over the limit? Exercise indoors, not outside
  • Kids, elders, pregnant and ill family stay in; keep medicines ready
  • Frequent cough, hard breathing or tight chest: see a doctor
  • No open burning of rubbish or leaves; less charcoal grilling
