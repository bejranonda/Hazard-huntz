Cleaning a water tank after a flood

🚰 Work out the chlorine

Pick the tank's size and the chlorine you have to see how much to use, with the steps one by one. Every line and its source is below.

🔒 Nothing is sent anywhere

Amounts, steps and the sources

  1. Liquid chlorine, 5% · 100 ml / 1,000 litres
    Sources: Metropolitan Waterworks Authority (MWA) · Department of Disease Control (DDC) (2025)
  2. Liquid chlorine, 10% · 50 ml / 1,000 litres
    Sources: Metropolitan Waterworks Authority (MWA) · Department of Disease Control (DDC) (2025)
  3. Chlorine powder, 60–65% · 8 g / 1,000 litres
    Dissolve the powder in a bucket first; use only the clear water and throw the sludge away
    Sources: Metropolitan Waterworks Authority (MWA) · Department of Disease Control (DDC) (2025)
  4. Step by step
    Close the inlet valve, open every tap and drain the tank
    Scrub with a long-handled brush. Never climb in: you could run out of air
    Hose it down, drain it, then fill it nearly full
    Add the chlorine above and stir it in
    Let it stand: overnight, says the MWA; 3 hours, says the DDC
    Drain it out through the taps (it can wash floors), then refill and use as usual
    Sources: Department of Disease Control (DDC) (2025) · Metropolitan Waterworks Authority (MWA)
  5. Take care
    Wear rubber gloves
    Never mix chlorine or bleach with toilet cleaner: it makes a toxic gas
    Water standing with chlorine is not for drinking: drain it all
    Buy one whose label states its strength (%) ⚠️
    Sources: Department of Disease Control (DDC) (2025) · Thai Food and Drug Administration (FDA) (2023) · Metropolitan Waterworks Authority (MWA) · Department of Science Service (DSS) (2025) · ⚠️ being reviewed with the agency
