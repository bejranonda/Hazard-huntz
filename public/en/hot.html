Four levels, what to do, and the sources

  1. Watch · 27.0–32.9 °C
    Drink clean water often, without waiting to feel thirsty
    Keep an eye on heat warnings
    Sources: Department of Health (DOH) (2026) · Bangkok Metropolitan Administration (BMA) (2026) · Thai Meteorological Department (TMD) (2024)
  2. Warning · 33.0–41.9 °C
    Spend less time active in the sun
    Drink clean water often
    Feeling unwell? See a doctor soon
    Sources: Bangkok Metropolitan Administration (BMA) (2026) · Department of Health (DOH) (2026)
  3. Danger · 42.0–51.9 °C
    Keep out of the sun; move activities to a cooler time
    Watch yourself for symptoms
    Feeling unwell? See a doctor soon
    Sources: Department of Health (DOH) (2026) · Bangkok Metropolitan Administration (BMA) (2026)
  4. Extreme danger · 52.0+ °C
    Stop outdoor activity
    Feeling unwell? See a doctor soon
    Sources: Bangkok Metropolitan Administration (BMA) (2026) · Department of Health (DOH) (2026)
  5. People who need extra care
    Older people, young children, pregnant women, people with a long-term illness such as heart disease, high blood pressure or diabetes, outdoor workers, people with obesity, heavy drinkers
    Sources: Department of Health (DOH) (2026) · Bangkok Metropolitan Administration (BMA) (2026)
  6. Signs of heatstroke
    A very hot body, red skin, a racing pulse, a headache, dizziness, confusion, slurred speech, drowsiness or fainting
    Still sweating? It can still be heatstroke
    Sources: Department of Health (DOH) (2026) · Department of Medical Services (DMS) (2023)
  7. First aid
    Call 1669 at once
    Move them into the shade with moving air; lie them down with their feet raised
    Take off or loosen their clothes
    Cold wet cloths or ice at the neck, armpits and groin, and a fan
    Sources: Department of Health (DOH) (2026) · Department of Medical Services (DMS) (2023) · Bangkok Metropolitan Administration (BMA) (2026) · Department of Health (DOH) (2026)
