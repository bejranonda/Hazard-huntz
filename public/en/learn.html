Keeping the home safe in any disaster: the game's lessons, and what to do

Every lesson from the Baan Rod Mai? game (floods, PM2.5 dust, house fire and power cuts) and the short "what to do" guides for every hazard, including flash floods, storms, earthquakes and extreme heat, each with its source.

Most of this advice comes from Thai government agencies, and the helplines below are Thai numbers (199 is the Thai fire service). Most of it applies anywhere, but outside Thailand, follow your local authorities and call your local emergency number.

Before the flood

Sandbags for the door ถุงทรายหน้าประตู

Front yard · 🏠 Home, car & belongings

Do this: Line them up tightly across the doorway

Line sandbags tightly across the door to slow water getting in.

Why: DDPM advises lining sandbags into a barrier around the house; the Government PR Department says use sandbags or waterproof material to seal doors and windows.

Sources: Department of Disaster Prevention and Mitigation (DDPM) (2022) · Government Public Relations Department (PRD) (2026)

The car รถยนต์

Front yard · 🏠 Home, car & belongings

Do this: Move it to an official flood-parking point or a multi-storey car park

Move the car early to official flood parking, never onto bridges or ramps.

Why: DDPM says move vehicles to safe ground. During floods, car parks often open for free: check local announcements (Bangkok: BMA Flood Support). Police have asked people not to park on bridges or expressway ramps because it blocks patient transport.

Sources: Department of Disaster Prevention and Mitigation (DDPM) (2024) · Bangkok Metropolitan Administration (BMA) · Royal Thai Police (Metropolitan Police Bureau) (2011) · ⚠️ being reviewed with the agency

Plan for the pets แผนพาน้องหมาน้องแมว

Front yard · 🎒 Plans & go-bag

Do this: Pack a carrier or leash, a name-tag collar and food, and take them with you

Ready a carrier, leash, name-tag collar and food; take your pets when you go.

Why: DDPM says evacuate pets to safety. The Department of Health says shelters can take dogs and cats, which should be rabies-vaccinated, kept in a carrier or on a leash in shared areas, and wear a collar with a clear name tag.

Sources: Department of Disaster Prevention and Mitigation (DDPM) (2022) · Department of Health (DOH) (2026) · Department of Land Transport (DLT) (2026)

The nearest shelter ศูนย์พักพิงใกล้บ้าน

Front yard · 🎒 Plans & go-bag

Do this: Note the shelter and the official route, and tell everyone at home

Know where your nearest shelter is and the route officials recommend.

Why: The Government PR Department says follow the officially designated route to shelters; DDPM says evacuate by safe routes. Bangkok shelters are listed on BMA Flood Support; elsewhere follow provincial and local announcements, or call DDPM 1784.

Sources: Government Public Relations Department (PRD) (2026) · Department of Disaster Prevention and Mitigation (DDPM) (2022) · Bangkok Metropolitan Administration (BMA) · Department of Disaster Prevention and Mitigation (DDPM)

Street drain out front ตะแกรงท่อระบายน้ำหน้าบ้าน

Front yard · 🏠 Home, car & belongings

Do this: Clear the rubbish off the grate so rain can drain

Clear rubbish from street drains so rainwater can flow away.

Why: DDPM advises preventing house flooding by keeping rubbish out of pipes, drains and waterways so water can flow freely.

Sources: Department of Disaster Prevention and Mitigation (DDPM) (2022)

Important documents เอกสารสำคัญ

Living room · 🏠 Home, car & belongings

Do this: Seal them in a waterproof bag and keep it with the go-bag

Seal key documents in a waterproof bag and keep them in your go-bag.

Why: DDPM advises keeping important documents in plastic or waterproof pouches inside the emergency bag. If you evacuate, they prove who you are and help you get assistance.

Sources: Department of Disaster Prevention and Mitigation (DDPM) (2022) · Department of Disaster Prevention and Mitigation (DDPM) · Department of Disaster Prevention and Mitigation (DDPM) (2025)

Main breaker เบรกเกอร์ไฟหลัก

Living room · ⚡ Power & gas

Do this: Switch off the downstairs breaker now, while you're dry and on dry ground

Switch off the breaker before water gets in, only while dry and on dry ground.

Why: DDPM and the electricity authorities say to cut downstairs power as flooding approaches. MEA says stand on dry ground in rubber shoes while doing it; for single-storey homes PEA says stop using power entirely.

If water is already inside: Don't touch it! Get to dry ground and call 1130 / 1129 · Wet or in water? Don't touch switches. Get to dry ground and call 1130/1129.

Sources: Department of Disaster Prevention and Mitigation (DDPM) (2024) · DDPM and MEA (2025) · Provincial Electricity Authority (PEA) (2025) · Metropolitan Electricity Authority (MEA) (2022) · Provincial Electricity Authority (PEA) (2025) · Provincial Electricity Authority (PEA) (2025) · Metropolitan Electricity Authority (MEA) (2026) · Provincial Electricity Authority (PEA)

TV and appliances ทีวีและเครื่องใช้ไฟฟ้า

Living room · ⚡ Power & gas

Do this: Unplug it and carry it upstairs or somewhere high

Unplug appliances and lift them and valuables upstairs before the water arrives.

Why: DDPM says move belongings, especially appliances and valuables, above the flood level. PEA says do it before water enters, and don't plug in or use appliances where water can reach.

Sources: Department of Disaster Prevention and Mitigation (DDPM) (2022) · Provincial Electricity Authority (PEA) (2025) · DDPM and MEA (2025)

Phone and power bank มือถือและพาวเวอร์แบงก์

Living room · 🎒 Plans & go-bag

Do this: Charge both to full and save emergency numbers

Charge your phone and power bank fully; save 1784 and 1669 in your contacts.

Why: DDPM lists a phone, charger and power bank among emergency essentials. In deep floods the utility may cut power temporarily, so a charged phone is your lifeline.

Sources: Department of Disaster Prevention and Mitigation (DDPM) · Department of Disaster Prevention and Mitigation (DDPM) (2025) · Metropolitan Electricity Authority (MEA) (2026) · ⚠️ being reviewed with the agency

Torch and spare batteries ไฟฉายและถ่านสำรอง

Living room · 🎒 Plans & go-bag

Do this: Pack the torch and spare batteries in the go-bag

Keep a torch and spare batteries in the go-bag, ready for a power cut.

Why: A torch with spare batteries is one of DDPM's six emergency-bag essentials. It lights the way in a power cut and saves your phone battery for calls.

Sources: Department of Disaster Prevention and Mitigation (DDPM) · Department of Disaster Prevention and Mitigation (DDPM) (2025)

Grandma's care plan แผนดูแลคุณยาย

Living room · 🎒 Plans & go-bag

Do this: Move grandma somewhere safe early, with her medicine and aids

Move older and bedridden family members to safety early, with medicine and aids.

Why: DDPM says help children and older people first when evacuating. The Department of Health says keep regular medicine and mobility aids ready. PEA says move bedridden patients who rely on electricity to safety. When calling for help, tell responders an older or bedridden person is there.

Sources: Department of Disaster Prevention and Mitigation (DDPM) (2022) · Department of Health (DOH) · Provincial Electricity Authority (PEA) (2025) · Government Public Relations Department (PRD) (2026)

Water and dry food น้ำดื่มและอาหารแห้ง

Kitchen · 💧 Water & food

Do this: Stock water and dry food for at least 3 days, stored up high

Keep at least 3 days of water and dry food, sealed and stored up high.

Why: DDPM says keep long-life water and food for at least 3–5 days. The Department of Health says store it in sealed containers; the Government PR Department suggests at least 3 litres of drinking water per person per day.

Sources: Department of Disaster Prevention and Mitigation (DDPM) (2022) · Department of Disaster Prevention and Mitigation (DDPM) · Department of Health (DOH) (2025) · Government Public Relations Department (PRD) (2026)

LPG cylinder ถังแก๊สหุงต้ม

Kitchen · ⚡ Power & gas

Do this: Close the valve tight and tie the cylinder to something sturdy up high

Close the gas valve tight and tie the cylinder up high so it can't tip or float.

Why: The Government PR Department advises closing the cylinder valve tightly and tying the cylinder to a sturdy part of the house up high, so it can't tip or float away and leak.

Sources: Government Public Relations Department (PRD) (2026)

Medicines and first-aid kit ยาประจำตัวและชุดปฐมพยาบาล

Kitchen · 🦠 Health after floods

Do this: Get at least 7 days of regular medicine; pack it waterproof with the first-aid kit

Pack at least 7 days of regular medicine, waterproofed, with a first-aid kit.

Why: A first-aid kit and regular medicines are among DDPM's six emergency-bag essentials. The Ministry of Public Health advises people with chronic conditions to keep at least 7 days of medicine.

Sources: Department of Disaster Prevention and Mitigation (DDPM) · Ministry of Public Health (MOPH) (2013) · Department of Health (DOH) · ⚠️ being reviewed with the agency

Emergency numbers เบอร์โทรฉุกเฉิน

Kitchen · 🎒 Plans & go-bag

Do this: Save them in everyone's phone and keep a paper copy in the go-bag

Save 1784, 1669 and your electricity hotline on every phone, plus on paper.

Why: DDPM's 1784 safety hotline runs 24 hours; medical emergencies are 1669. For electricity: MEA 1130 (Bangkok, Nonthaburi, Samut Prakan) and PEA 1129 (other provinces). In Bangkok, the BMA is on 1555.

Sources: Department of Disaster Prevention and Mitigation (DDPM) · National Institute for Emergency Medicine (NIEMS) · Metropolitan Electricity Authority (MEA) (2026) · Provincial Electricity Authority (PEA) · Bangkok Metropolitan Administration (BMA) (2026) · ⚠️ being reviewed with the agency

Chemicals under the sink สารเคมีใต้ซิงก์

Kitchen · 🏠 Home, car & belongings

Do this: Cap them tightly and move them up high, away from food

Cap pesticides and chemicals tightly and store them high, away from food.

Why: Chemical containers can leak or float off in floodwater. The Department of Disease Control warns about flooded chemical containers and says never pour chemicals into drains. Capping and lifting them first reduces contamination.

Sources: Department of Disease Control (DDC) (2025) · ⚠️ being reviewed with the agency

Kitchen floor drain ท่อระบายน้ำที่พื้นครัว

Kitchen · 🏠 Home, car & belongings

Do this: Plug it with a sandbag or a water-filled bag to stop backflow

Plug floor drains with a sandbag to stop sewage backflow; unplug after.

Why: The Government PR Department advises plugging downstairs toilets and drains so sewage can't push back into the house. A sandbag or water-filled bag is an easy plug; remove it once the water drops.

Sources: Government Public Relations Department (PRD) (2026) · ⚠️ being reviewed with the agency

Downstairs toilet โถส้วมชั้นล่าง

Bathroom · 🏠 Home, car & belongings

Do this: Press a plastic-wrapped sandbag into the bowl and close the lid

Press a plastic-wrapped sandbag into the downstairs toilet to stop backflow.

Why: The Government PR Department advises plugging downstairs toilets and drains to stop sewage pushing back into the house.

Sources: Government Public Relations Department (PRD) (2026) · ⚠️ being reviewed with the agency

Fuel and weedkiller in the garage น้ำมันและยาฆ่าหญ้าในโรงรถ

Garage · 🏠 Home, car & belongings

Do this: Seal them tight and move them up onto a high shelf

Seal fuel, fertiliser and weedkiller and store them high, out of floodwater.

Why: The Department of Disease Control warns floodwater can carry fuel or gas, urges care with flooded chemical containers, and says never pour chemicals into drains. Storing them sealed and high beforehand limits contamination.

Sources: Department of Disease Control (DDC) (2025) · ⚠️ being reviewed with the agency

After the water recedes

Downed power line สายไฟขาดห้อยลงมา

Front yard · ⚡ Power & gas

Do this: Stay at least 3–5 m away and call 1130 / 1129

See a downed line? Stay 3–5 m away and call 1130 or 1129 right away.

Why: PEA says keep at least 3–5 m from hanging or broken lines and leaning poles because current can leak into the ground. The Department of Disease Control says leaks can spread at least 3 m. Report to PEA 1129 or MEA 1130.

Sources: Provincial Electricity Authority (PEA) (2025) · Department of Disease Control (DDC) (2024) · Metropolitan Electricity Authority (MEA) (2026)

Shoes by the door รองเท้าหน้าบ้าน

Front yard · 🐍 Snakes & sharp things

Do this: Check inside with a stick before putting them on

Snakes and centipedes hide in shoes. Check with a stick first, never bare hands.

Why: The Department of Disease Control warns snakes, centipedes and scorpions shelter in homes and dark corners after floods. Check clothes and shoes before wearing them, use a stick, never bare hands. If bitten, keep the limb still, no tourniquet, and get to hospital or call 1669.

Sources: Department of Disease Control (DDC) (2024) · Department of Disease Control (DDC) (2025) · Department of Disease Control (DDC) (2021) · Ministry of Public Health (MOPH) (2019)

About to wade the mud in flip-flops จะลุยโคลนด้วยรองเท้าแตะ

Front yard · 🦠 Health after floods

Do this: Rubber boots and gloves; afterwards wash with soap and dry off

Boots and gloves in mud, then wash with soap and dry off to avoid leptospirosis.

Why: The Department of Disease Control says leptospirosis enters through cuts, scratches, eyes, nose, mouth or water-softened skin. If you must wade, wear boots and rubber gloves, cover wounds with waterproof plasters, then wash with soap and dry off at once.

Sources: Department of Disease Control (DDC) (2024) · Department of Disease Control (DDC) (2026) · Department of Disease Control (DDC) (2025) · Department of Disease Control (DDC) (2026)

Glass and nails in the mud เศษแก้วกับตะปูในโคลน

Front yard · 🐍 Snakes & sharp things

Do this: Thick-soled boots and heavy gloves; scoop it into a strong bag

Sharp debris hides in mud: thick-soled boots, heavy gloves, a scoop, not hands.

Why: The Department of Disease Control warns that cuts from glass and sharp objects can get infected. Clear nails and sharp debris from floors and paths, wear boots with puncture-proof soles, and clean and cover any cut with a waterproof plaster.

Sources: Department of Disease Control (DDC) (2025) · Department of Disease Control (DDC) (2025) · Department of Disease Control (DDC) (2025) · Department of Disease Control (DDC) (2025)

A car that was under water รถที่จมน้ำมา

Front yard · 🏠 Home, car & belongings

Do this: Don't start it; take photos and have it towed for inspection

Never start a flooded car. Photograph the damage and have it towed for checks.

Why: The Department of Land Transport says never start a car that was under water: water in the electrics can short-circuit and wreck the engine. Once the water drops, have it lifted to a service centre for a full check. The insurance regulator (OIC) says photograph the outside, inside, engine bay and water marks for your claim. In Bangkok, traffic police tow flooded cars for free on 1197.

Sources: Department of Land Transport (DLT) (2026) · Office of Insurance Commission (OIC) (2026) · Traffic Police Division (2026)

Pile of flood debris กองเศษขยะที่น้ำพัดมา

Front yard · 🐍 Snakes & sharp things

Do this: Boots and gloves on; poke it with a long stick first, then clear it

Debris piles can hide centipedes or snakes. Check with a long stick, not hands.

Why: The Department of Disease Control says flood sediment, rubbish bags and crevices can hide venomous animals. Check with a stick, never bare hands, use a torch for dark corners, and never burn chemicals.

Sources: Department of Disease Control (DDC) (2025) · Department of Disease Control (DDC) (2024)

Cracked wall ผนังบ้านร้าว

Front yard · 🏠 Home, car & belongings

Do this: Don't move back in yet; have a builder or engineer check it

Cracked walls, sunken floors or a lean? Don't move in until it's been checked.

Why: The Department of Health says check the structure is sound before going back in. The Department of Public Works and Town & Country Planning's post-flood repair manual says wide, long or through-wall cracks, cracked beams or a sinking, tilting foundation need an engineer.

Sources: Department of Health (DOH) (2022) · Department of Public Works and Town & Country Planning (DPT)

Standing water in a bucket น้ำขังในถัง

Front yard · 🦠 Health after floods

Do this: Tip it out, rinse it and turn it upside down

Tip out standing water and turn containers over so mosquitoes can't breed.

Why: The Department of Disease Control warns flooding keeps dengue cases coming. Cover stored water tightly, rinse and upturn containers, and check rubbish for standing water so Aedes mosquitoes can't breed.

Sources: Department of Disease Control (DDC) (2021) · Department of Disease Control (DDC) (2024) · Department of Disease Control (DDC) (2026)

Stagnant water out front น้ำเน่าขังหน้าบ้าน

Front yard · 🦠 Health after floods

Do this: Clear the rubbish, then help drain it so the water can move

EM balls don't treat stagnant floodwater. Drain it, clear rubbish, add air.

Why: 48 environmental scientists (statement, 9 Nov 2011) don't recommend EM for stagnant floodwater: it adds no oxygen and can add dirt. Drain the water first, remove rubbish, then add air. A 2026 study also found EM balls alone didn't help consistently. In Bangkok, report wide-area standing water to 1555 or Traffy Fondue.

Sources: 48 environmental scientists (Dept. of Environmental Engineering, Chulalongkorn University) (2011) · Bangkokthonburi University (2026) · Bangkok Metropolitan Administration (BMA) (2026) · ⚠️ being reviewed with the agency

Wet socket ปลั๊กไฟที่เปียก

Living room · ⚡ Power & gas

Do this: Don't touch or use it until an electrician has checked it

A socket that was under water: don't touch or use it until it's been checked.

Why: MEA warns never to use sockets, appliances or switches that were flooded until an expert has checked them. The Department of Disease Control says don't touch wet sockets or wires.

Sources: Metropolitan Electricity Authority (MEA) (2022) · Metropolitan Electricity Authority (MEA) (2026) · Department of Disease Control (DDC) (2026)

Breaker panel after the flood ตู้เบรกเกอร์หลังน้ำลด

Living room · ⚡ Power & gas

Do this: Call an electrician, or the electricity authority, to check before switching on

After a flood, don't touch the breaker on a wet floor. Get an electrician first.

Why: The Department of Disease Control says don't inspect or switch the electrical system yourself. PEA says never stand in water while checking equipment and have a skilled electrician check before reconnecting. MEA 1130 and PEA 1129 are open 24 hours.

Sources: Department of Disease Control (DDC) (2026) · Provincial Electricity Authority (PEA) (2025) · Metropolitan Electricity Authority (MEA) (2026)

TV that was under water ทีวีที่จมน้ำมา

Living room · ⚡ Power & gas

Do this: Don't plug it in; let it dry and have a technician check it

Flooded appliances: don't plug in, don't self-repair. A technician checks first.

Why: PEA says don't use flooded appliances straight away, don't reuse or repair them yourself, and have a skilled electrician check them first.

Sources: Provincial Electricity Authority (PEA) (2025) · Provincial Electricity Authority (PEA) (2025)

Waterlogged ceiling ฝ้าเพดานอุ้มน้ำ

Living room · 🏠 Home, car & belongings

Do this: Keep out from under it, cordon it off and get a builder to replace it

A sagging wet ceiling can fall in one piece. Keep clear; a builder removes it.

Why: The Department of Public Works and Town & Country Planning's manual says gypsum board can't take water: flooded ceilings usually have to be stripped out and replaced because they turn soft. A soaked board is heavy and weak, so stay out from under it and keep the lights off until it's checked.

Sources: Department of Public Works and Town & Country Planning (DPT) · Department of Disease Control (DDC) (2026) · ⚠️ being reviewed with the agency

Mould on the wall เชื้อราบนผนัง

Living room · 🦠 Health after floods

Do this: N95 mask and rubber gloves on, doors and windows open, then clean

Mould: N95 mask, rubber gloves, windows open, and no fans while you clean.

Why: The Department of Disease Control says mould triggers allergies and asthma: wear an N95, don't dry-wipe or blow fans at it. The Department of Health says open doors and windows and keep fans and AC off while removing mould. People with asthma or allergies, children and older people shouldn't do the cleaning.

Sources: Department of Disease Control (DDC) (2025) · Department of Health (DOH) (2022)

Generator running indoors เครื่องปั่นไฟในบ้าน

Living room · ⚡ Power & gas

Do this: Switch it off and move it outside into open air

Never run a generator indoors or in closed spaces: its fumes are silent poison.

Why: The Department of Disease Control warns against running fuel-powered pumps or generators in poorly ventilated or enclosed spaces because of toxic gas. Carbon monoxide has no colour or smell, so it harms people without warning.

Sources: Department of Disease Control (DDC) (2025)

Fever after wading มีไข้หลังลุยน้ำ

Living room · 🦠 Health after floods

Do this: See a doctor soon and say you waded through floodwater

Fever and sore calves after wading? See a doctor; don't self-medicate.

Why: The Department of Disease Control says leptospirosis shows up 2–30 days after exposure, usually 1–2 weeks: sudden high fever, headache, muscle pain especially in the calves, red eyes. Don't self-medicate; see a doctor and mention wading. With dengue, NSAIDs like aspirin and ibuprofen must be avoided.

Sources: Department of Disease Control (DDC) (2024) · Department of Disease Control (DDC) (2026) · Department of Disease Control (DDC) (2024) · Department of Disease Control (DDC) (2026)

One towel for the whole family ผ้าเช็ดตัวผืนเดียวใช้ทั้งบ้าน

Living room · 🦠 Health after floods

Do this: Give everyone their own towel, wash hands often, don't rub eyes

Red eyes after a flood? Wash hands often, don't rub, don't share towels.

Why: The Department of Disease Control lists eye infections (conjunctivitis) among six disease groups that come with floods. Wash hands often, don't rub your eyes, and don't share towels or personal items.

Sources: Department of Disease Control (DDC) (2026)

Gas smell in the kitchen กลิ่นแก๊สในครัว

Kitchen · ⚡ Power & gas

Do this: No switches, no flames: open windows, close the valve, go outside and call

Smell gas? No switches or flames. Open windows, shut the valve, leave and call.

Why: DDPM says if gas leaks, close the cylinder and stove valves, open every door and window, don't switch any electrical device on or off or make sparks, and never use a fan. The Department of Disease Control adds: use a battery torch when re-entering, as floodwater can carry gas or fuel. Get clear of the area before you call for help.

Sources: Department of Disaster Prevention and Mitigation (DDPM) (2022) · Department of Disease Control (DDC) (2025)

Food that touched floodwater อาหารที่โดนน้ำท่วม

Kitchen · 💧 Water & food

Do this: Throw it all out, even if it's bagged or boxed

Food that touched floodwater goes in the bin, even if it was bagged or boxed.

Why: The Department of Disease Control says don't eat food that touched floodwater, even in wet or damaged packaging. The Department of Health says throw out flood-damaged rice and dry food because floodwater carries germs, and eat freshly cooked food: cooked, hot, clean.

Sources: Department of Disease Control (DDC) (2026) · Department of Health (DOH)

Dented, bulging, rusty cans กระป๋องบุบ บวม เป็นสนิม

Kitchen · 💧 Water & food

Do this: Throw out any can that's dented, bulging, leaking or rusty

Dented, bulging, leaking or rusty cans: throw them out, don't eat them.

Why: The Thai FDA lists dented, bulging or rusty cans as unsafe to eat. The Department of Health says check cans are sealed, not swollen or rusty, within date, and never heat food in the can.

Sources: Thai Food and Drug Administration (FDA) (2020) · Department of Health (DOH) (2021)

Diarrhoea after the flood ท้องเสียหลังน้ำลด

Kitchen · 🦠 Health after floods

Do this: Sip ORS often to stay hydrated, then eat freshly cooked food

Diarrhoea after a flood? Sip ORS to stay hydrated; eat freshly cooked food.

Why: The Department of Disease Control lists stomach and bowel illness among six disease groups that come with floods. Eat freshly cooked food, drink clean water, and if diarrhoea starts, drink oral rehydration salts (ORS) to prevent dehydration.

Sources: Department of Disease Control (DDC) (2026)

Tap water after the flood น้ำประปาหลังน้ำลด

Kitchen · 💧 Water & food

Do this: Run it until clear and boil before drinking, or drink standard bottled water

After a flood, run taps clear and boil before drinking, or use bottled water.

Why: The Department of Health says boil tap water before drinking and choose standard bottled water; don't drink or cook with water you suspect is contaminated. The waterworks say the mains supply stays safe, but flooded household plumbing should be checked and tanks cleaned before use.

Checklists to share

10 things to do before the flood

  1. Seal key documents in a waterproof bag in your go-bag
  2. Water and dry food for at least 3 days
  3. At least 7 days of regular medicine, plus first aid
  4. Torch, spare batteries, phone and power bank charged
  5. Lift appliances, valuables and chemicals up high
  6. Breaker off while dry; gas valve shut, cylinder tied up high
  7. Sandbag the doors; plug downstairs toilets and drains
  8. Move the car to official parking, never onto bridges
  9. Get older people, bedridden relatives and pets out early
  10. Know your nearest shelter; save 1784 and 1669

10 checks before going back in

  1. Check the structure first: cracks, sinking floors, sagging ceilings
  2. Rubber boots and gloves whenever you wade water or mud
  3. Don't touch wet sockets, switches or the breaker; call an electrician
  4. Don't plug in flooded appliances or start a flooded car
  5. Downed line: stay 3–5 m away and call 1130 or 1129
  6. Smell gas: no switches or flames, open windows, leave and call
  7. Check shoes, cupboards and debris with a stick for snakes
  8. Bin flood-touched food and damaged cans; boil tap water
  9. Never mix bleach and toilet cleaner; no generators indoors
  10. Fever after wading? See a doctor; don't self-medicate

  3. Smell gas? Shut the cylinder valve, open doors and windows
    Sources: Department of Mineral Resources (DMR) (2025)
  4. Wear closed shoes against broken glass and debris
    Sources: Department of Mineral Resources (DMR) (2025)

10 things for a fire-safe home

  1. Power strips with the TIS 2432 mark; never chain them
  2. Cracked, hot or burning-smelling cord? Stop and unplug
  3. Don't charge phones on beds or sofas
  4. Stay with the pan; turn the stove off if you leave
  5. After cooking, close the cylinder valve and the stove
  6. Keep flammables away from the stove and heat
  7. Keep an extinguisher in sight, ready to grab
  8. No open burning, especially on windy days
  9. In a fire, touch the door first; if hot, keep it shut
  10. Wet cloth over nose and mouth, crawl low, get out, call 199

10 things for a 3-day power cut

  1. Drinking water: 3 litres per person per day, for 3 days
  2. Dry and canned food for 3 days; eat it and restock
  3. A manual can opener, kept with the food
  4. A torch for everyone, with spare batteries
  5. A battery radio for official news
  6. Phones and power banks kept charged
  7. 7 days of daily medicine; a plan for cold medicine
  8. Keep the fridge shut: about 4 hours for fresh food
  9. Unplug appliances against surges
  10. Generators outdoors only; torches, not candles

Get ready

    Sources: Department of Disease Control (DDC) (2025) · Department of Disease Control (DDC) (2025) · Department of Health (DOH) (2022) · Ready.gov (U.S. FEMA) (2026)

10 things to do on PM2.5 days

  1. Check the dust level in Air4Thai before going out
  2. High-dust day: shut doors and windows, seal the gaps
  3. Run a purifier; check and change the filter every 6 months
  4. Wipe with a damp cloth instead of sweeping
  5. Wet-wipe fans, air-cons, window screens and bedding
  6. Going out? A snug N95 over the nose and under the chin
  7. Dust over the limit? Exercise indoors, not outside
  8. Kids, elders, pregnant and ill family stay in; keep medicines ready
  9. Frequent cough, hard breathing or tight chest: see a doctor
  10. No open burning of rubbish or leaves; less charcoal grilling

Thai emergency helplines

  • 1784 DDPM disaster hotline: Report disasters, ask for help, 24 h
  • 199 Fire: A fire you can't put out: get out, then call the fire service
  • 1669 Medical emergency (NIEMS): Ambulance, 24 h
  • 1555 Bangkok (BMA): Report problems and get help in Bangkok
  • 1130 MEA electricity: Bangkok, Nonthaburi, Samut Prakan
  • 1129 PEA electricity: The other 74 provinces
  • 1323 Mental health hotline: Stressed or worn out after the flood? Free, 24 h

Most of this advice comes from Thai government agencies, and the helplines below are Thai numbers (199 is the Thai fire service). Most of it applies anywhere, but outside Thailand, follow your local authorities and call your local emergency number.
